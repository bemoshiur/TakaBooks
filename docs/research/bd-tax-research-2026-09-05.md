### Why AY 2026-27 is the operative year (worked through, not assumed)

1. Bangladesh's income year for individuals and most companies is 1 July – 30 June (Income Tax Act 2023; confirmed by KPMG p.13 and p.44).
2. The assessment year is the twelve months commencing 1 July following the end of the income year.
3. The most recently completed income year is 1 July 2025 – 30 June 2026. Its assessment year began 1 July 2026 → **AY 2026-27**.
4. The Finance Act 2026 legislates rates **for AY 2026-27 through AY 2030-31** (a first for Bangladesh — a five-year rate card). So the Finance Act 2026 governs both the return now being filed *and* the year now running.
5. Cross-check: NBR opened the e-Return portal for "the 2026-27 tax year" on 22 July 2026 and the early-filing rebate window runs 1 July – 30 September 2026 — i.e. right now.

**The "cashless"/bank-transfer condition — stated precisely.** The 2.5-percentage-point reduction is available to both listed and non-listed general companies (categories 1–3 only) and requires **100% bank-transfer compliance across all transactions for the whole income year**. As described in the sources, this means:

- **all** types of income and receipts must be received through bank transfer; and
- **all** expenses and investments — each single transaction exceeding **Tk 500,000**, and in annual aggregate exceeding **Tk 3,600,000** — must be made through bank transfer.

A single non-compliant cash transaction forfeits the reduced rate for the entire year (TNP p.24). **⚠️ The Tk 500,000 / Tk 3,600,000 figures are the long-standing statutory thresholds carried forward and are corroborated only by secondary commentary for FY2026-27 — see UNVERIFIED.** PwC and KPMG both describe the condition simply as "all transactions through bank transfer" without restating the thresholds, and KPMG notes as an FA 2026 change that "cashless operation provisions now require **all** transactions to be conducted through bank transfers."

### 1.7 Minimum tax / final tax — a major structural change

**The rule that treated tax deducted at source under ss.89–151 as a non-refundable minimum tax has been WITHDRAWN** (ITA 2023 ss.163(1)-(2), 164). Loss-making and thin-margin businesses whose withholding exceeded their regular liability can now obtain **refunds**. NBR's own illustration: a supplier with Tk 5 crore of source tax against a Tk 2.5 crore regular liability now pays Tk 2.5 crore and receives a Tk 2.5 crore refund. (TNP p.17; PwC p.3; KPMG p.4)

**Left the final-tax net** (now regular taxation): profit on savings certificates (sanchayapatra); capital gains on compensation for compulsorily acquired property; export cash subsidies/incentives; capital gains on transfer of property.
**Remain / newly final:** tax collected from operation of water vessels (newly final); tax collected from commercial motor vehicles (final, but if regular-basis tax is higher the higher amount is payable); withholding under Part 7 for persons exempt from filing under s.166(2); motor-vehicle advance tax under s.138; **withholding suffered by non-residents having no PE in Bangladesh**. (TNP p.17; PwC p.12–13)

**Exclusion:** capital gains **up to Tk 5,000,000** are excluded from income where earned on transfer of shares or units of a listed company, or of any fund approved by BSEC, **by persons other than sponsors, directors or placement shareholders** (KPMG p.49).

**Capital asset definition widened (s.2(77))** to include personal holdings of gold, silver and other precious metals, gold/silver ornaments, gems, diamonds, coins, paintings, antiques, digital coins and **club memberships**. Business inventory and vehicles for personal use remain excluded. (TNP p.7; KPMG p.52; PwC p.6)

**Firm-to-company conversion (s.61(4)-(6))** — capital gains exemption codified with four conditions: (i) all assets and liabilities transfer to the company; (ii) partners become shareholders in the same capital proportions; (iii) partners receive no consideration other than shares; (iv) existing partners retain at least 50% of voting power for five years. Accumulated losses and unabsorbed depreciation carry over. Breach withdraws the exemption. (TNP p.10)

**Indirect transfers**: capital gains tax applies to transfer of shares in a foreign company that directly or indirectly holds Bangladesh interests, apportioned by fair value; Offshore Indirect Transfer Rules 2022 govern computation (KPMG p.53).

### 1.9 Filing deadlines, incentives and additional tax — RESTRUCTURED

The Finance Act 2026 replaced the old fixed "Tax Day + 2% per month penalty" model with a sliding window. **The NBR's power to extend the return filing date by one month has been REPEALED.**

**(a) Individuals and Hindu Undivided Families — ITA 2023 s.170(1)**

| Filing window (income year ended 30 June) | Consequence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **1 July – 30 September** | **Rebate: 5% of tax payable, capped at Tk 25,000** | PwC p.7; KPMG p.45; TNP p.18 |
| **1 October – 31 December** | Neutral — no rebate, no additional tax | same |
| **1 January – 31 March** | Additional tax: **higher of 2% of tax payable or Tk 3,000** | same |
| **1 April – 30 June** | Additional tax: **higher of 5% of tax payable or Tk 5,000** | same |

**Practical effect: the effective individual deadline is now 31 December** (previously "Tax Day" = 30 November). NBR's own press release for AY 2026-27 states: *"if the return is filed between July and September, taxpayers will get an incentive of up to 5% on the net tax payable, with a maximum limit of Tk 25,000… if the return is submitted after December, additional tax will have to be paid."* (Dhaka Tribune, 22 July 2026 — https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/415724/nbr-begins-e-return-submission-service-for-2026-27)

**As of 5 September 2026, individuals are inside the 5% early-filing rebate window for AY 2026-27, which closes 30 September 2026.**

**(b) Companies and other entities — ITA 2023 s.170(2)**

Due date = **the later of (i) the 15th day of the ninth month following the end of the income year, and (ii) 15 September following the end of the income year** (KPMG p.13).

- For a 30 June 2026 year-end → **due date 15 March 2027**.
- For banks/insurance/finance companies with a 31 December 2025 year-end → **15 September 2026**.

| Filing window | Consequence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| More than 2 months **before** the due date | Rebate: lower of 5% of tax under s.173(2) or Tk 25,000 | KPMG p.13; PwC p.11; TNP p.18 |
| Within the final 2 months up to the due date | Neutral | same |
| After the due date but within the assessment year (to 30 June) | Additional tax: **higher of 2% of tax payable or Tk 25,000** | same |

Foreign companies get a shorter 15-day incentive window at the start of their cycle (TNP p.18).

**(c) Delayed returns beyond the tax year — ITA 2023 s.174.** The old 2%-per-month (max 24 months) charge is replaced:

| Situation | Additional tax | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Voluntary (suo motu) delayed return | Higher of **10%** of net tax payable or **Tk 5,000** | PwC p.14; TNP p.18 |
| Return filed in response to a notice under s.212(1) (reassessment) | Higher of **15%** of tax payable or **Tk 10,000** | same |

NBR's illustration: on the same facts, additional tax falls from Tk 40,200 to Tk 8,375 (TNP p.18).

**(d) Special-circumstance returns (s.172):** business closure or ownership transfer during the year (listed companies excepted), persons leaving Bangladesh, evasion cases, change in a partnership's constitution, business or firm succession. Specified Bangladeshi individuals returning from abroad must file within **90 days** of return (PwC p.6; TNP p.19).

**(e) Return completeness.** A return is treated as incomplete/defective — and can be cancelled under s.176(3), i.e. treated as never filed — if it is not accompanied by the acknowledgement copy of the **withholding tax return**, or if the Statement of Assets and Liabilities (IT-10B) or Statement of Lifestyle Expenses (IT-10BB) is defective and a s.176(2) resubmission notice is not complied with (PwC p.13; TNP p.19).

**(f) Universal self-assessment (s.170Ka)** is now **mandatory for individuals**. Automated refunds are available to individuals whose income comprises only employment, financial assets and/or agriculture: DCT processes within **120 days** of filing and the refund is paid electronically within **60 days** of the refund application. Any other income head (e.g. house property) routes the return to assessment under s.183 instead. (PwC p.6; TNP p.18)

### 1.11 Investment tax rebate — ITA 2023 s.78 (REDUCED by Finance Act 2026)

**Rebate = the LOWEST of:**

| Limb | AY 2026-27 | Previous | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) 3% of total taxable income (excluding income subject to exemption, reduced rate or final tax, and income from a firm or AOP) | 3% | 3% | KPMG p.47 |
| (b) Percentage of actual eligible investment | **10%** | 15% | PwC p.6; KPMG p.47; TNP p.12 |
| (c) Absolute ceiling | **Tk 750,000** | Tk 1,000,000 | PwC p.6; KPMG p.47; TNP p.12 |

TNP's practice note: taxpayers must now invest roughly 50% more to obtain the same rebate; top-bracket taxpayers lose up to Tk 250,000 of maximum rebate.

**Eligible investments (Sixth Schedule Part 3)** — per KPMG p.47:

- Life insurance premium
- Contribution to an **approved** provident fund (both employee and employer contribution). **New under FA 2026: the PF must be a *recognised* PF for the contribution to qualify** (PwC p.6)
- Contribution to a deposit pension scheme / monthly savings scheme, maximum **Tk 120,000**, sponsored by a scheduled bank or financial institution
- Donation to a national-level institution set up in memory of the Liberation War
- Donation to a government-approved public welfare or educational institution
- Donation to a Zakat Fund / charitable fund established by or under the Zakat Fund (FA 2026 added donations to the **Centre for Zakat Management**)
- Investment in government securities up to **Tk 500,000**

- Any new sum invested in shares of listed companies
- **Donations to eleven named charities**, 1 July 2026 – 30 June 2030 (SRO 213-AIN/Income Tax-4/2026): ASHIC Foundation for Childhood Cancer; Bangladesh Cancer Aid Trust (BANCAT); Al-Markazul Islami; Disabled Child Foundation; Sherpur Diabetic Association; Mawna/Magura Diabetic Association *(sources differ on the name)*; Bangladesh Thalassemia Society; Autism Welfare Foundation; BRAC; Ramakrishna Math and Ramakrishna Mission, Dhaka; Chattogram Maa-O-Shishu Hospital

### 1.13 Proof of Submission of Return (PSR) — ITA 2023 s.264

**What counts as PSR** (KPMG p.30): (a) acknowledgement receipt of the income tax return; (b) a system-generated tax certificate; or (c) a certificate issued by the Deputy Commissioner of Taxes — each containing the name, TIN and the assessment year for which the return was submitted. For certain services a system-generated certificate containing only name and TIN may be submitted in lieu of PSR. Persons not required to submit a return are excluded.

**Finance Act 2026 changes** (TNP p.19): the ambit of PSR is "rationalised"; PSR is **newly required** from resident **directors and sponsor shareholders** of companies, from **candidates for Union Parishad chairman**, and from **educational institutions seeking renewal**; taxpayers must **display their latest PSR at their business premises**; and a TIN is mandatory for registration, transfer and fitness renewal of **motorcycles of 150cc and above**.

### 1.14 Mandatory return filing / exemption from filing (KPMG p.29)

**Filing is compulsory for every person who:** (1) earns income exceeding the exemption threshold; (2) was assessed for tax in any one of the three immediately preceding income years; (3) is a company; (4) is a shareholder director or shareholder employee of a company; (5) is a firm; (6) is a partner of a firm; (7) is an association of persons; (8) is an employee holding an executive or management position in a business or profession; (9) is a public servant; (10) is a non-resident having a PE in Bangladesh; (11) is subject to tax exemption or a lower tax rate; (12) is required to be registered as a taxpayer; (13) is required to furnish PSR.

**Filing is NOT mandatory for:** primary/pre-primary schools teaching the Bengali-version curriculum, government secondary/higher secondary schools, and MPO institutions without an English-version curriculum; public universities; Bangladesh Bank; local authorities; BTRC; BSEC; Cantonment Boards; statutory bodies not carrying on commercial activity and receiving regular government funding; orphanages and religious institutions; funds; **non-resident individuals having no PE in Bangladesh** (new under FA 2026); and any class of persons authorised by the NBR.

### 1.15 Other Finance Act 2026 income tax changes worth flagging

- **Digital PE (s.2(92)):** a non-resident with **100,000 or more digital/online customers or subscribers** in Bangladesh has a permanent establishment.
- **Loans/advances/deposits to individuals (s.67(13)):** aggregate receipts exceeding **Tk 500,000** are taxed as income from other sources if received outside banking channels; or, if received through banking channels from a person other than a bank/finance/leasing company, if unpaid for **six years**.
- **Interest on loans to associated enterprises:** if charged below **12% p.a.**, the difference is taxed as business income (banks, finance and leasing companies excluded).
- **Associated enterprise definition:** cumulative debt threshold lowered from **50% to 35%** of book value of total assets.
- **Rental income (ss.35–39):** warehouse rent brought into the rent head; salami/premium moved from other sources into rental income; adjustable advances and refundable deposits — **10% of the year-end closing balance** included annually as "special rental income"; non-adjustable advances spread over the rental tenure or five years, whichever is lower; refundable deposits not refunded within six years taxed as other income.
- **Depreciation:** maximum depreciable value of motor vehicles raised from **Tk 3,000,000 to Tk 6,000,000**. Accelerated depreciation outside Dhaka and Chattogram city corporations now **60% year 1 / 40% year 2** (previously 50/30/20 over three years), extended to tourism and sports ventures; NBR application window extended from 6 to 12 months.
- **Expense disallowance thresholds:** excess perquisite Tk 2,000,000 → **Tk 2,500,000**; entertainment expense simplified to 4% of the first Tk 1,000,000 of computed business income; pharmaceutical sample expense 2% up to Tk 100m turnover / 1.5% Tk 100–200m / 1% above Tk 200m; promotional expenses other than advertisement 0.5% → **1% of business turnover**.
- **Withholding default penalty (major change):** default in deducting or depositing withholding tax on business/profession and agriculture payments now results in **additional tax of 150% of the defaulted tax, with NO disallowance of the related expenditure** (previously the expense was disallowed). The same 150% applies to defaults on payments for acquisition of capital assets.
- **Appeal pre-deposits slashed:** Commissioner (Appeals) 0% → **1%** of disputed tax (10% where no return was filed); Taxes Appellate Tribunal 10% → **3%**; High Court Division 15%/25% → **10%**. **Waiver facilities are withdrawn** — the smaller deposit is now unavoidable.
- **Assessment time limits tightened** to the assessment year plus 12 months (previously two years); arm's-length price determinations extend to five years.
- **Additional tax for employing a non-Bangladeshi citizen without approval:** 50% of tax payable or Tk 500,000, whichever is higher — retained, but the taxpayer must now be heard first.
