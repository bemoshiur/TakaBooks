### Why AY 2026-27 is the operative year (worked through, not assumed)

1. Bangladesh's income year for individuals and most companies is 1 July – 30 June (Income Tax Act 2023; confirmed by KPMG p.13 and p.44).
2. The assessment year is the twelve months commencing 1 July following the end of the income year.
3. The most recently completed income year is 1 July 2025 – 30 June 2026. Its assessment year began 1 July 2026 → **AY 2026-27**.
4. The Finance Act 2026 legislates rates **for AY 2026-27 through AY 2030-31** (a first for Bangladesh — a five-year rate card). So the Finance Act 2026 governs both the return now being filed *and* the year now running.
5. Cross-check: NBR opened the e-Return portal for "the 2026-27 tax year" on 22 July 2026 and the early-filing rebate window runs 1 July – 30 September 2026 — i.e. right now.

**The "cashless"/bank-transfer condition — stated precisely.** The 2.5-percentage-point reduction is available to **both listed and non-listed general companies** (categories 1–3 only) and requires **100% bank-transfer compliance across all transactions for the whole income year**. The two conditions, as stated by PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries (reviewed 31 July 2026, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/bangladesh/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income):

1. *"All transactions to be undertaken through bank transfer"*; and
2. *"All income is received through bank transfer."*

A single non-compliant cash transaction forfeits the reduced rate for the entire year (TNP p.24). KPMG records as an explicit **FA 2026 change** that "cashless operation provisions now require **all** transactions to be conducted through bank transfers" and that "this benefit can be availed by both listed and non-listed general companies" (KPMG p.19).

⚠️ **Important nuance — the old de-minimis thresholds may be gone.** Under the pre-FA 2026 rule the condition was qualified: expenses/investments had to be routed through banking channels where a **single transaction exceeded Tk 500,000** or the **annual aggregate exceeded Tk 3,600,000**. Some secondary commentary still repeats those figures for FY2026-27, but **PwC (both publications) and KPMG state the condition without any threshold, and KPMG flags the move to "all transactions" as the change made by the Finance Act 2026.** Treat the condition as an unqualified 100% requirement and verify against the enacted text before relying on any de-minimis. ### 1.7 Minimum tax / final tax — a major structural change

**The rule that treated tax deducted at source under ss.89–151 as a non-refundable minimum tax has been WITHDRAWN** (ITA 2023 ss.163(1)-(2), 164). Loss-making and thin-margin businesses whose withholding exceeded their regular liability can now obtain **refunds**. NBR's own illustration: a supplier with Tk 5 crore of source tax against a Tk 2.5 crore regular liability now pays Tk 2.5 crore and receives a Tk 2.5 crore refund. (TNP p.17; PwC p.3; KPMG p.4)

**Left the final-tax net** (now regular taxation): profit on savings certificates (sanchayapatra); capital gains on compensation for compulsorily acquired property; export cash subsidies/incentives; capital gains on transfer of property.
**Remain / newly final:** tax collected from operation of water vessels (newly final); tax collected from commercial motor vehicles (final, but if regular-basis tax is higher the higher amount is payable); withholding under Part 7 for persons exempt from filing under s.166(2); motor-vehicle advance tax under s.138; **withholding suffered by non-residents having no PE in Bangladesh**. (TNP p.17; PwC p.12–13)

### 1.9 Filing deadlines, incentives and additional tax — RESTRUCTURED

The Finance Act 2026 replaced the old fixed "Tax Day + 2% per month penalty" model with a sliding window. **The NBR's power to extend the return filing date by one month has been REPEALED.**

**(a) Individuals and Hindu Undivided Families — ITA 2023 s.170(1)**

| Filing window (income year ended 30 June) | Consequence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **1 July – 30 September** | **Rebate: 5% of tax payable, capped at Tk 25,000** | PwC p.7; KPMG p.45; TNP p.18 |
| **1 October – 31 December** | Neutral — no rebate, no additional tax | same |
| **1 January – 31 March** | Additional tax: **higher of 2% of tax payable or Tk 3,000** | same |
| **1 April – 30 June** | Additional tax: **higher of 5% of tax payable or Tk 5,000** | same |

> ⚠️ **CRITICAL NUANCE — the statutory due date and the penalty-free window are NOT the same date.**
>
> The term **"Tax Day" (কর দিবস) has been replaced** by **"রিটার্ন দাখিলের নির্দিষ্ট তারিখ"** — *specified date for filing return* — inserted as **s.2(80A)** by **Act 89 of 2026 §29(ট)**. Read from bdlaws, s.2(80A) sets:
>
> | Taxpayer | Specified date |
> |---|---|
> | Individual and Hindu Undivided Family | **30 November** following the end of the income year |
> | Any other taxpayer (companies etc.) | **15th day of the ninth month** following the income year end; or, where that falls before 15 September, **15 September** |
> | Individual who has **never filed before** | **30 June** following the income year end |
> | Individual residing abroad (higher education, deputation or lien, or valid visa and work permit) | **90th day** from the date of return to Bangladesh |
> | Extension | A date approved by the **Commissioner of Taxes** on written application made before the specified date, for unavoidable reasons — **not more than 90 days** beyond the individual dates |
> | Public holiday | The immediately following working day |
>
> So for an individual the **specified date is 30 November**, but the additional-tax ladder above imposes **no charge until 1 January**. The 1 October – 31 December band is genuinely penalty-free in cash terms, yet a return filed on, say, 10 December is still filed **after the specified date** — which matters for other provisions keyed to that date (notably the s.162 advance-tax interest uplift and "delayed return" characterisation). **Do not treat 31 December as the statutory deadline.** NBR's own press release for AY 2026-27 says only: *"if the return is filed between July and September, taxpayers will get an incentive of up to 5% on the net tax payable, with a maximum limit of Tk 25,000… if the return is submitted after December, additional tax will have to be paid"* (Dhaka Tribune, 22 July 2026 — https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/415724/nbr-begins-e-return-submission-service-for-2026-27).
>
> ⚠️ Note also a possible conflict: PwC states the **NBR's** one-month extension power is repealed, yet **s.2(80A)(e)** gives the **Commissioner of Taxes** a 90-day extension power for individuals. Whether the Finance Act 2026 removed s.2(80A)(e) is **unconfirmed** — see UNVERIFIED.

**As of 5 September 2026, individuals are inside the 5% early-filing rebate window for AY 2026-27, which closes 30 September 2026.**

**(b) Companies and other entities — ITA 2023 s.170(2)**

Due date = **the later of (i) the 15th day of the ninth month following the end of the income year, and (ii) 15 September following the end of the income year** (KPMG p.13).

- For a 30 June 2026 year-end → **due date 15 March 2027**.
- For banks/insurance/finance companies with a 31 December 2025 year-end → **15 September 2026**.

Note: the "ninth month" formulation is **not** new in the Finance Act 2026 — the NBR's Income Tax Paripatra for AY 2025-26 already fixed the company deadline at 15 March (https://vatax.bd/company-tax-return-submission-deadline/). What the Finance Act 2026 changed is the incentive/additional-tax structure around it and the repeal of the NBR's one-month extension power.

| Situation | Additional tax | Source |
|---|---|---|

**(d) Special-circumstance returns (s.172):** business closure or ownership transfer during the year (listed companies excepted), persons leaving Bangladesh, evasion cases, change in a partnership's constitution, business or firm succession. Specified Bangladeshi individuals returning from abroad must file within **90 days** of return (PwC p.6; TNP p.19).

**(e) Return completeness.** A return is treated as incomplete/defective — and can be cancelled under s.176(3), i.e. treated as never filed — if it is not accompanied by the acknowledgement copy of the **withholding tax return**, or if the Statement of Assets and Liabilities (IT-10B) or Statement of Lifestyle Expenses (IT-10BB) is defective and a s.176(2) resubmission notice is not complied with (PwC p.13; TNP p.19).

**(f) Universal self-assessment (s.170Ka)** is now **mandatory for individuals**. Automated refunds are available to individuals whose income comprises only employment, financial assets and/or agriculture: DCT processes within **120 days** of filing and the refund is paid electronically within **60 days** of the refund application. Any other income head (e.g. house property) routes the return to assessment under s.183 instead. (PwC p.6; TNP p.18)

### 1.14 Mandatory return filing / exemption from filing (KPMG p.29)

**Filing is compulsory for every person who:** (1) earns income exceeding the exemption threshold; (2) was assessed for tax in any one of the three immediately preceding income years; (3) is a company; (4) is a shareholder director or shareholder employee of a company; (5) is a firm; (6) is a partner of a firm; (7) is an association of persons; (8) is an employee holding an executive or management position in a business or profession; (9) is a public servant; (10) is a non-resident having a PE in Bangladesh; (11) is subject to tax exemption or a lower tax rate; (12) is required to be registered as a taxpayer; (13) is required to furnish PSR.

**Filing is NOT mandatory for:** primary/pre-primary schools teaching the Bengali-version curriculum, government secondary/higher secondary schools, and MPO institutions without an English-version curriculum; public universities; Bangladesh Bank; local authorities; BTRC; BSEC; Cantonment Boards; statutory bodies not carrying on commercial activity and receiving regular government funding; orphanages and religious institutions; funds; **non-resident individuals having no PE in Bangladesh** (new under FA 2026); and any class of persons authorised by the NBR.

### 1.15 Other Finance Act 2026 income tax changes worth flagging

- **Digital PE (s.2(92)):** a non-resident with **100,000 or more digital/online customers or subscribers** in Bangladesh has a permanent establishment.
- **Loans/advances/deposits to individuals (s.67(13)):** aggregate receipts exceeding **Tk 500,000** are taxed as income from other sources if received outside banking channels; or, if received through banking channels from a person other than a bank/finance/leasing company, if unpaid for **six years**.
- **Interest on loans to associated enterprises:** if charged below **12% p.a.**, the difference is taxed as business income (banks, finance and leasing companies excluded).
- **Associated enterprise definition:** cumulative debt threshold lowered from **50% to 35%** of book value of total assets.
- **Rental income (ss.35–39):** warehouse rent brought into the rent head; salami/premium moved from other sources into rental income; adjustable advances and refundable deposits — **10% of the year-end closing balance** included annually as "special rental income"; non-adjustable advances spread over the rental tenure or five years, whichever is lower; refundable deposits not refunded within six years taxed as other income.
- **Depreciation:** maximum depreciable value of motor vehicles raised from **Tk 3,000,000 to Tk 6,000,000**. Accelerated depreciation outside Dhaka and Chattogram city corporations now **60% year 1 / 40% year 2** (previously 50/30/20 over three years), extended to tourism and sports ventures; NBR application window extended from 6 to 12 months.
- **Expense disallowance thresholds:** excess perquisite Tk 2,000,000 → **Tk 2,500,000**; entertainment expense simplified to 4% of the first Tk 1,000,000 of computed business income; pharmaceutical sample expense 2% up to Tk 100m turnover / 1.5% Tk 100–200m / 1% above Tk 200m; promotional expenses other than advertisement 0.5% → **1% of business turnover**.
- **Withholding default penalty (major change) — ITA 2023 s.55(Ka) repealed in effect; new s.56(1)(Ka) & (Kha):** previously, failing to deduct tax at source meant the **entire expense was disallowed** (an effective cost of ~27.5% of the payment). That is abolished. Now:
  - **s.56(1)(Ka):** the deductor pays the undeducted or short-deducted tax **plus a 50% additional amount — i.e. 1.5× the shortfall (150% of the defaulted tax in total)**;
  - **s.56(1)(Kha):** where s.143 recovery proceedings have run (which can add the 50% uplifts for non-banking-channel payment or missing PSR, plus **interest at 2% per month capped at 24 months**) and the amount collected is less than the clause (Ka) benchmark, the deductor pays the difference plus 50% of that difference;
  - **the expense itself remains fully deductible**, and the old "special business income" characterisation under the previous s.56 is abolished.
  - NBR's worked example: the cash cost of a missed 5% deduction on a Tk 1,000 expense falls from Tk 275 to roughly Tk 75–148. (TNP p.9; PwC p.13)
- **Appeal pre-deposits slashed:** Commissioner (Appeals) 0% → **1%** of disputed tax (10% where no return was filed); Taxes Appellate Tribunal 10% → **3%**; High Court Division 15%/25% → **10%**. **Waiver facilities are withdrawn** — the smaller deposit is now unavoidable.
- **Assessment time limits tightened** to the assessment year plus 12 months (previously two years); arm's-length price determinations extend to five years.
- **Additional tax for employing a non-Bangladeshi citizen without approval:** 50% of tax payable or Tk 500,000, whichever is higher — retained, but the taxpayer must now be heard first.

### 2.14 The PSR / "TIN" uplift — s.142

⚠️ The rule is framed around **proof of submission of return (PSR)**, *not* around possession of a TIN.

| Rule | Trigger | Effect | Section |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PSR uplift** | Payee is **required to furnish PSR and fails to** | Rate is **50% higher than the applicable rate** | **s.142(1)** |
| **Bank-transfer uplift** | Payee **does not receive** the contract value, bill, rent, fee, charge, remuneration, salary or other payment **through bank transfer** | Rate is **50% higher than the applicable rate** | **s.142(2)** |

**Carve-outs from s.142(1):**
- s.142(1) opens *"ধারা ১১৯ এর ব্যতিক্রম সাপেক্ষে"* — **subject to the exception of s.119** — so payments to **non-residents are outside it**.
- **Proviso** (added by Act 89 of 2026 s.88): does **not** apply to a person for whom filing is not mandatory under **s.166(2)**, or a person exempted by the Board from furnishing PSR under **s.264(4)**.

**Gross-up (s.141 / Rule 12):** for a tax-free payment, C = (100 × A) / (100 − B), where A = the tax-free payment and B = the applicable rate; where the payee fails PSR or does not take payment by bank transfer, **B is the uplifted rate**.

> ⚠️ **Whether the two 50% uplifts STACK is genuinely unresolved.** Both sub-sections independently say the rate "shall be 50% higher than the applicable rate" (প্রযোজ্য হার), and neither cross-refers to the other. Whether they compound (to +100%, or to 2.25×) is ambiguous on the face of the statute. **There is no stated maximum**, and neither SRO 273 nor the Paripatra 2026-27 addresses it — no NBR worked example applies either uplift, let alone both. Seek a ruling before relying on a specific combined rate.

### 2.15 Minimum / final tax character — s.163 REPLACED, s.164 REPEALED

**The biggest structural change of FA 2026.** The marginal note of s.163 is now *"অগ্রিম কর, চূড়ান্ত কর এবং টার্নওভার ট্যাক্স"*.

| Provision | Effect | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Withholding in **excess** of assessed tax is **REFUNDABLE**, or may be carried forward and adjusted against prior/subsequent years' liability | The old broad minimum-tax character of withholding is abolished | s.163(1)–(2) |
| **Only** tax under **s.138 (commercial motor vehicles)** and **s.139 (inland vessels)** is **final tax** for those sources | | s.163(3) |
| …but if tax at the applicable rate on the **declared income** exceeds that final tax, the **higher** amount is payable | | s.163(5) |
| For a person **exempt from filing under s.166(2)**, **all** Part 7 withholding is final tax | | s.163(4) |
| Non-resident **without a PE** — withholding is final tax | | s.119(3) |
| **s.164 repealed** (excess/short withholding not to be minimum tax) | | FA 2026 s.92 |

**No longer final/minimum tax:** profit on **sanchayapatra**; capital gain on compensation for compulsory acquisition; capital gain on transfer of property; **cash incentive/subsidy against export**.

> ⚠️ **A sequencing trap on s.163.** **Act 89 of 2026 §94** (April 2026) rewrote s.163 and, in its s.163(2), listed certain withholdings — including the 10% on WPPF payouts under s.88 — as **minimum tax**. **Act 96 of 2026 (the Finance Act 2026) §91 then replaced s.163 again**, with effect from 1 July 2026, and §92 repealed s.164. The Finance Act 2026 version is the later and operative one, and its marginal note is *"অগ্রিম কর, চূড়ান্ত কর এবং টার্নওভার ট্যাক্স"* — advance tax, final tax and turnover tax — with **no "minimum tax" limb**. **Sources describing a s.163(2) minimum-tax list are reading the superseded April 2026 version.** Verify against the current consolidated text before treating any withholding as minimum tax.

### 2.16 Deposit deadlines — Rule 9 of SRO 273

All deposits must be made **through A-Challan** (এ-চালানের মাধ্যমে).

| Row | When deducted / collected | Deposit by |
|---|---|---|
| ক | Any deduction/collection in **July to May** of the financial year | Within **2 weeks from the end of the month** of deduction/collection |
| খ | Any day from the **1st to the 20th day of June** | Within **7 days** following the day of deduction/collection |
| গ | **Any other day of June** | The **next day** |
| ঘ | The **last working day of June** | The **same day** |

Row ঘ verbatim: *"অর্থবৎসরের জুন মাসের **শেষ কর্মদিবসে** কর কর্তন বা সংগ্রহের ক্ষেত্রে"* → *"কর্তন বা সংগ্রহের দিন"*.

> ⚠️ Note the statute says **last *working* day of June**, not "30 June". This matters when 30 June falls on a weekend or public holiday. KPMG's published summary renders rows গ/ঘ as "21–29 June / 30 June", which is a simplification of the actual rule.

**Enabling provision:** s.146(1) — deducted/collected amounts must be deposited within the prescribed time or as directed by the Board. s.146(2) forbids anyone from withholding tax otherwise than under the Act.

### 3.3 Who must register — s.4

**s.4(1):** registrable from the first day of a month if turnover in the preceding 12 months exceeded the registration threshold, **or** estimated turnover for the next 12 months will exceed it.

**s.4(2)** (substituted by Finance Act 2022 s.57) — registration is mandatory **irrespective of turnover** for a person who: (a) supplies, manufactures or imports **supplementary-duty-liable** goods or services; (b) supplies goods/services **through a tender, contract or work order**; (c) is engaged in **import-export** business; (d) establishes a **branch, liaison or project office of a foreign entity**; (e) is appointed a **VAT agent**; (f) operates in a **Board-specified geographic area** or in specified goods/services. The registration threshold does not apply to these persons (proviso to s.2(57)).

**s.4(3) — NEW, inserted by Finance Act 2026 s.3, effective 1 July 2026.** A BIN or proof of enlistment is **mandatory** for: (a) opening/operating a **current or STD account** with a bank/NBFI/FI; (b) taking a **loan** from a bank/NBFI/FI; (c) **trade licence renewal**; (d) opening an **MFS merchant account**; (e) obtaining/renewing **trade association membership**; (f) **electricity and gas connections**; (g) **BRTA vehicle registration** in the business's name.

**s.10 — replaced by Finance Act 2026 s.4:** a person crossing the enlistment threshold (but not the registration threshold) within 12 months must apply **within 30 days** through the **eVAT system**; if in order, eVAT **auto-approves and issues the enlistment certificate immediately** (তাৎক্ষণিকভাবে).

Sources: http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1106/section-42297.html ; http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1106/section-42303.html

**Central vs unit registration — s.5** (http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1106/section-42298.html):

- **s.5(1)** (subst. FA 2022 s.58): a person supplying **identical or similar** goods/services from one or more places who keeps **all** accounts, tax payment and records **centrally in a software-based automated system** may take **one central registration**. **Proviso:** even for identical/similar supplies, if any unit keeps its accounts and records **separately**, a **separate registration is required for each unit**.

| (chha) | Supply from an importer where the importer's invoice omits the **goods declaration number**, or the description does not properly match commercially |
| (ja) | Inputs released against **bank guarantee**, until final settlement. Proviso (Act 89 of 2026 s.5(a)(iii)): credit allowed within **six tax periods** from the later of bank-guarantee release or final duty/tax payment |
| (jha) | Input tax on goods/services used to produce **exempt** goods or supply exempt services |
| (yna) | **Turnover tax** paid |
| (ta) | **Supplementary duty** paid on inputs |

| (ta²) | Input tax on inputs/goods declared under s.32(6) — **new**, FA 2026 s.8(a)(iii) |

**s.46(2) — absolute disallowances.** No ITC on an acquisition or import that: **(ka)** relates to **passenger vehicles**, their **spare parts**, or their **repair/maintenance services** — unless dealing in, hiring out or providing transport with vehicles is part of the person's economic activity **and** the vehicle was acquired for that purpose; **(kha)** relates to or is used for **entertainment** — unless providing entertainment is part of the economic activity and is given in the normal course; **(ga)** relates to **membership or right of entry to a sports, social or recreational club, society or association**. Clause **(gha) was DELETED by Finance Act 2026 s.8(b)**.

**s.46(3) — documents required to support an ITC claim:** (ka) import — the **goods declaration** bearing the importer's name, address and BIN; (kha) supply — the **tax invoice (Mushak 6.3)**; (gha) for s.20(2) — the **treasury challan** (substituted FA 2026 s.8(c)); (uma) for **gas, water, electricity, bank, insurance, port and telephone** services — the provider's bill, treated as an invoice; (cha) for electricity bills — invoices issued by banks, MFS providers and digital payment gateways, treated as invoices.

**Definition of "input" — s.2(18A).** The word **"শ্রম" (labour)** was **DELETED from the negative list by Finance Act 2026 s.2(a)**, effective 1 July 2026, so labour and manpower-supply costs can now qualify as an input and carry ITC. The remaining negative list (NOT inputs): land; buildings; office equipment and fixtures; construction, renovation, modernisation, replacement, extension or repair of buildings or infrastructure; all furniture, office supplies, stationery, refrigerators and freezers, air conditioners, fans, lighting equipment, purchase or repair of generators; interior design and architectural planning/design; purchase, rent and lease of vehicles; travel, entertainment, employee welfare, development work and related goods/services; and rent of business premises, office, showroom or similar space.

**Partial credit:** **s.47** (আংশিক উপকরণ কর রেয়াত) governs apportionment where a person makes both taxable and exempt supplies — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1106/section-42343.html

**Late return (s.65):** on application, the Commissioner may permit late filing, but this **cannot extend the actual tax payment date by more than 1 month** and **does not alter interest liability** (http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1106/section-42361.html).

(http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1106/section-42362.html ; KPMG p.70)

The BIN is automatically unlocked **within two days** of the return being submitted. (KPMG p.70)

### 4.1 Companies Act 1994, s.181 — books of account

**s.181(1) — required books.** Every company shall keep proper books of account with respect to:

| Clause | Required records |
|---|---|
| (ক) | All sums of money received and expended by the company, and the matters in respect of which the receipt and expenditure took place |

**s.181(2):** books are **not** deemed proper unless they give a **true and fair view** of the company's affairs and contain a **sufficient explanation of its transactions**.

**s.181(3) — place of keeping.** The books must be kept at the company's **registered office** and be open to inspection by the directors at all times during business hours.
> **Proviso:** by a **decision of the board of directors**, all or any of the books may be kept **at any other place in Bangladesh for not more than SIX MONTHS**, and where the board so decides the company must, **within SEVEN DAYS** of the decision, file written notice with the **Registrar** giving the **full address** of that other place.

**s.181(4) — branch offices.** Where a company has a branch office in or outside Bangladesh, s.181(1) is deemed complied with if proper books of the transactions effected at that office are kept there **and summarised returns, made up to date, are sent by the branch to the registered office (or the other place under s.181(3)) at intervals of not more than THREE MONTHS**.

**s.181(5) — ⭐ RETENTION PERIOD.** Every company shall **preserve in good order the books of account, and the vouchers relevant to any entry in them, for a period of NOT LESS THAN TWELVE YEARS immediately preceding the current year**. *Proviso:* a company incorporated less than twelve years before the current year must preserve them for the **whole period since incorporation**.

**s.181(6) — penalty.** A person in s.181(7) who fails to take reasonable steps to secure compliance, or by his own wilful act causes a default, is punishable **for each offence** with imprisonment up to **six months**, or a fine up to **Tk 5,000**, or both.

**s.181(7) — who is liable.** (ক) where the company has a managing agent, managing director, executive director, general manager or manager — that person and **all other officers of the company**, but **excluding the manager's bankers, the auditors and the legal advisers**; (খ) where the managing agent is a firm — **every partner**; (গ) where the managing agent is a body corporate — **every director** of it; (ঘ) where there is no managing agent, managing director, executive director, general manager or manager — **every director** of the company.

| **Financial year length** | The period covered is the "financial year" and may be **more or less than a calendar year, but NOT MORE THAN FIFTEEN MONTHS** — *proviso:* extendable **up to eighteen months** with the Registrar's special permission | **s.183(4)** |
| **Directors' report** | Board's report to accompany the accounts | **s.184** |
| **Holding/subsidiary financial year** | Alignment rules | **s.187** |
| **Unsigned balance sheet** | Issuing a balance sheet not signed / not accompanied as required — imprisonment up to 6 months, or fine up to Tk 2,000, or both | **s.189** |

**⭐ s.36 — annual list of members and summary (the "annual return" / Schedule X).**

- **s.36(1):** every company having a share capital shall, **within eighteen months of incorporation and thereafter at least once in every year**, prepare a list in the form set out in **SCHEDULE X** of persons who were members on the day of the first or only general meeting of that year, and of those who ceased to be members since the last return.
- **s.36(2):** contents — names, addresses, nationality and occupation of all past and present members; the number of shares held by each present member; dates of registration of transfers; and a **summary** of share capital, shares taken, amounts called up, amounts received on calls, amounts unpaid, and commissions or discounts allowed on shares or debentures and not written off.
- **⭐ s.36(3) — DEADLINE:** the list and summary must be contained in a **separate part of the register of members** and **completed within TWENTY-ONE DAYS after the first or only general meeting of the year**; the company must then **forthwith** file with the **Registrar** a copy of that part, signed by **two directors including the managing director** (or, where there is no managing director, by one director) **and by the managing agent, manager or secretary**, together with their **certificate** that the list and summary state the facts correctly as at the date of the return.
- **s.36(4):** a **private company** must additionally file a certificate signed by a director or officer that no invitation to the public to subscribe for shares or debentures has been issued since the last return (or since incorporation for the first return); and where the return discloses **more than fifty members**, a certificate that the excess persons are outside the fifty-member limit under s.2(1)(ট)(ই).
- **s.36(5) — penalty:** fine up to **Tk 200 for every day** during which the contravention continues, on the company **and** every officer who knowingly and wilfully authorises or permits it.

**⭐ s.190 — filing the balance sheet with the Registrar.**

- **s.190(1) — DEADLINE:** within **THIRTY DAYS** from the date the balance sheet and profit and loss (or income and expenditure) account are **laid before the AGM** — or, where no AGM was held in a year, within **thirty days from the last date on or before which the AGM should have been held** — the company must file with the Registrar **THREE COPIES** of the balance sheet and P&L, signed by the **managing director, managing agent, manager or secretary** (or, if there is no such officer, by **a director**), together with all documents required by the Act to be annexed or attached.
  - *Proviso 1:* for a **private company**, copies of the balance sheet and of the profit and loss account must be filed with the Registrar **separately**.
  - *Proviso 2:* for a **private company that is not a subsidiary of a public company**, **no person other than a member** is entitled to inspect or obtain a copy of its profit and loss account.
- **⭐ s.190(1A)** *(inserted amendment)*: **a company established as a "public interest entity" as defined in section 2(8) of the Financial Reporting Act 2015 may NOT file its financial statements unless those statements were prepared following the standards made by the Financial Reporting Council under section 40 of that Act.**
- **s.190(2):** if the balance sheet laid before the AGM was **not adopted**, or no AGM was held, a **statement of that fact and the reasons** must be annexed to the balance sheet and to every copy filed with the Registrar.
- **s.190(3) — penalty:** fine up to **Tk 100 for every day** during which the failure continues, on the company and on every officer who knowingly and wilfully authorises or permits it.

**Foreign companies:** Part X (ss.379–387) governs foreign companies carrying on business in Bangladesh; **s.384** penalises non-compliance with fine up to **Tk 5,000** plus **Tk 500 per day** of continuing default.

**⭐ AGM detail — s.81.** Every company shall hold, in **each Gregorian calendar year**, a general meeting as its AGM and specify it as such in the notice, with **not more than fifteen (15) months** between one AGM and the next. *First proviso:* the **first AGM** may be held **within eighteen (18) months of incorporation**, and if so held no AGM is needed in the year of incorporation or the following year. *Second proviso:* on application to the **Registrar within thirty (30) days** of expiry of the prescribed period, the Registrar may extend the time for an AGM (**not the first AGM**) by **not exceeding ninety (90) days**, or **not beyond 31 December** of the calendar year to which the AGM relates, **whichever is earlier**. **s.82 penalty for failure to hold an AGM: fine up to Tk 10,000, and for continuing default a further fine up to Tk 250 for every day** after the first.

Also: **s.183(2)(b)(ii)** allows **12 months** (rather than 9) where the company carries on business or has interests **outside Bangladesh**, and the **Registrar may extend by up to three (3) months** on application made before expiry. **s.183(6):** a copy of the balance sheet, P&L/I&E and directors' report must be kept at the registered office for member inspection **at least fourteen (14) days before** the general meeting. **s.191:** a copy must be **sent free of charge, not less than fourteen (14) days before the meeting**, to every member, debenture holder and debenture trustee. **s.189:** authentication — for a non-banking company, signed by the managing agent, manager or secretary (if any) **and by not less than two directors, one of whom must be the managing director if there is one**; **s.189(3): the Board must approve the accounts before they are signed and before they go to the auditors for report.**

### 4.3 Audit — appointment, qualification, and who needs one

**Which entities need a statutory audit?** Under **s.183(3)** the balance sheet and profit and loss account laid before the AGM **must be audited** — and s.183 applies to **every company** holding an AGM under s.81, with no small-company exemption. **So statutory audit applies to all companies registered under the Companies Act 1994, including private limited companies.**

**Appointment — s.210:**

| Sub-s. | Rule |
|---|---|
| 210(1) | Every company shall, **at each AGM**, appoint one or more auditors to hold office **from the conclusion of that meeting until the conclusion of the next AGM**, and shall **within SEVEN DAYS** of the appointment give notice of it to every auditor appointed |
| 210(2) | Every auditor so appointed shall, **within THIRTY DAYS** of receiving notice of appointment from the company, **inform the Registrar in writing** whether he has accepted or refused the appointment |
| 210(3) | A retiring auditor must be re-appointed at the AGM unless specified exceptions apply |
| 210(4) | If no auditor is appointed at the AGM, **the Government may appoint** a suitable person to fill the vacancy |
| 210(5) | The company must notify the Government **within seven days** of the Government's power becoming exercisable; failure — fine up to **Tk 1,000** on the company and every responsible officer |
| 210(6) | The **first auditor(s)** must be appointed by the board **within ONE MONTH of the date of registration** of the company, holding office until the conclusion of the first AGM |

**Qualification — s.212(1):** **a person shall not be appointed as auditor of any company unless he is a "Chartered Accountant" within the meaning of the Bangladesh Chartered Accountants Order 1973 (P.O. No. 2 of 1973).** s.212(2) and (3) list disqualifications; **s.212(4):** an auditor who becomes disqualified after appointment is **deemed to have vacated office**. **s.213** sets out auditors' powers and duties.

**Audit requirement under income tax law — ITA 2023 s.73** (see also 1.15). Audited financial statements must accompany the return for:

| Category | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Any **company** | No threshold |
| Any person having income from **long-term contracts** | No threshold |
| Any **firm, association of persons, Hindu Undivided Family or artificial juridical person** | **turnover over Tk 100,000,000 (Tk 10 crore)** OR **capital over Tk 50,000,000 (Tk 5 crore)** |
| Special sectors added by FA 2026 | Gold, silver and gems importers, suppliers and warehouse operators; and manufacturers, importers, suppliers, distributors and retailers covered by the new s.130Ka |

> ⚠️ **PwC's *Finance Act 2026: Key Amendments* prints these thresholds as "turnover exceeding BDT 10m or capital exceeding BDT 5m"** — a factor-of-ten discrepancy. **KPMG (*Bangladesh Tax 2026* p.31) and Tuhin & Partners both give Tk 100m / Tk 50m**, and this document follows them. Verify before relying on it.

Financial statement components are **aligned with IAS/IFRS** — statement of comprehensive income, statement of financial position, statement of changes in equity, statement of cash flows, and notes. An **Income Computation Sheet** must also be filed with the return, certified by a **chartered accountant, cost and management accountant or income tax practitioner** (s.73, s.169(2)), and the **s.177 withholding return acknowledgement** must be attached.

**Penalties for defective audit reports (ITA 2023):** an incorrect or false audit report by a chartered accountant — not less than **Tk 50,000**, not more than **Tk 200,000** (**s.273**); furnishing a **fake audit report** — **Tk 100,000** for that income year (**s.274**); criminal liability for furnishing a fake audit report on verification — imprisonment **6 months to 5 years** (**s.316**).

### 4.4 Financial Reporting Act 2015 and the Financial Reporting Council

**Financial Reporting Act 2015 — Act No. 16 of 2015**; full text http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1169.html. The **FRC** was established in 2016 as a statutory body under the Finance Division, Ministry of Finance. **s.3** establishes the Council; **s.7** its objectives; **s.8** its powers and functions; **s.40** empowers it to make reporting and auditing standards; **s.54** constitutes the Appellate Authority.

**⭐ "Public interest entity" (জনস্বার্থ সংস্থা) — FRA 2015, s.2(8)**, read verbatim from the primary text.

**(a) An entity satisfying ANY ONE of:**

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| (অ) | a **bank company** as defined in **s.5(ণ) of the Bank Company Act 1991** |
| (আ) | any **securities issuer** with a reporting obligation to the Securities and Exchange Commission under the **Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission Act 1993** |
| (ই) | a **financial institution** as defined in **s.2(খ) of the Financial Institutions Act 1993** |
| (ঈ) | a **microcredit institution** as defined in **s.2(21) of the Microcredit Regulatory Authority Act 2006** |
| (উ) | an **insurer** as defined in **s.2(25) of the Insurance Act 2010** |
| (ঊ) | an entity whose **annual revenue in the preceding financial year exceeded the limit fixed by the Council by Gazette notification** |
| (ঋ) | an entity satisfying **ANY TWO** of, at the end of the preceding financial year: (1) it employs **at least the minimum number of persons prescribed by regulations**; (2) **total assets** exceed the Council's Gazette limit; (3) **total liabilities excluding shareholders' equity** exceed the Council's Gazette limit |

**(b) Also included where they meet the (a) criteria:** state-owned companies or commercial entities; **statutory authorities**; **NGOs** conducting voluntary activities in the private sector; and any other similar entity or institution.

| **Total assets** — (ঋ)(2) | **Tk 30 crore (BDT 300,000,000)** | **S.R.O. No. 35-Ain/2023** | same |
| **Total liabilities excluding shareholders' equity** — (ঋ)(3) | **Tk 10 crore (BDT 100,000,000)** | **S.R.O. No. 36-Ain/2023** | same |

Index of FRC instruments: https://frc.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dece933eb65569e1d7f8

"Manpower" (Reg. 2(1)(ক)) = persons working in and drawing salary from, or sharing the profit of, the entity, employed on a **permanent, temporary, casual, contractual or outsourcing** basis. Reg. 3(2)–(4): FRC may call for the manpower list, appointment rules and register, salary register, payroll, accounts, financial statements, audit statements and PF/gratuity records; after verification it **enlists the entity as a PIE**.

> ⚠️ **OBSOLETE FIGURES IN WIDE CIRCULATION.** FRC Notification **179/FRC/FRM/Prokgapon/2020-01** (11 March 2020, gazetted 29 June 2020) set revenue at **Tk 5 crore**, assets **Tk 3 crore** and liabilities excluding equity **Tk 1 crore**. **Those are superseded.** Many advisers and 2020 press reports still quote them — a ten-fold understatement that would wrongly sweep small companies into PIE status.

### 4.5 Accounting standards in Bangladesh — the naming change, resolved

**⭐ The "BFRS / BAS" naming was dropped with effect from annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2018.**

**ICAB Circular Ref: 1/1/ICAB-2017, dated 14 December 2017**, "Adoption and Publication of International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) and IFRS for SMEs", verbatim:

> "The Council of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Bangladesh (ICAB) has decided to adopt the International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) and IFRS for SMEs and **to publish IFRS (instead of BFRS) and IFRS for SMEs (instead of BFRS for SMEs) which will be effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2018**."

ICAB signed the supply agreement with the IFRS Foundation on **25 September 2017**. Source: https://www.icab.org.bd/icabadmin/uploads/ckeditor/7869Circular%20for%20Adoption%20&%20Publication%20of%20IFRS%20and%20IFRS%20for%20SMEs.pdf

**So the standards are cited in Bangladesh today by their international names — IFRS, IAS, IFRIC, SIC, IFRS for SMEs. The "BAS 1 / BFRS 15" style is legacy.**

**Who sets them now — the FRC:**

| Provision / instrument | Effect |
|---|---|

| **FRA 2015 s.40(4)** | The Council **may make a separate simplified financial reporting framework and standards for small and medium-sized entities**, consistent with international good practice |
| **FRA 2015 s.41** | The Council may **exempt small entities** from the simplified standard |
| **FRA 2015 s.43** | Standards must be pre-published for **60 days** of comment on the FRC website plus notice on **3 consecutive days in one Bangla and one English national daily** |
| **FRA 2015 s.69 (transitional)** | Standards adopted by the professional accountancy body remain in force **as if made under this Act** until the Council makes its own |

| **FRC, 2024** | Adopted **International Valuation Standards (IVS)** |
| **FRC 2026 notifications** | **IFRS 18 and IFRS 19 adopted — mandatory for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027**; **IFRS S1 and S2 (sustainability) permitted**; and four **prescribed auditor's report formats** — **mandatory for financial years ending 31 December 2026 or later** — whose signature block ends with a mandatory `DVC: ______` line |

**Income tax cross-references:** **ITA 2023 s.72(4)** — a **company** shall maintain accounts and prepare financial reports in accordance with **IAS, IFRS and IFRS for SMEs** and the relevant laws in force in Bangladesh ("IFRS for SMEs" inserted by Act 89 of 2026 s.55). **ITA 2023 s.73(4)** — a **developer or real estate developer shall follow all conditions of IFRS and shall record revenue in accordance with IFRS 15**.

> ⚠️ **Still unresolved: which SMEs / non-PIEs must apply IFRS for SMEs rather than full IFRS.** FRC adopted IFRS for SMEs in November 2020 for PIEs, and FRA ss.40(4)/41 empower a separate simplified framework with small-entity exemptions — but **no FRC instrument setting quantified applicability criteria was found**. The IFRS Foundation's Bangladesh profile (September 2020) records it as "under review, not yet finalised", and nothing later was located.

### 4.6 The s.73 audit requirement — the enacted text

**ITA 2023 s.73 was SUBSTITUTED IN FULL by the Finance Act 2026 (Act 96 of 2026) s.59, with effect from 1 July 2026.** Marginal note: *"কোম্পানি, ইত্যাদি কর্তৃক নিরীক্ষাকৃত আর্থিক প্রতিবেদন এবং আয় পরিগণনাপত্র দাখিল"*.

| Sub-s. | Requirement (current, from 1 July 2026) |
|---|---|
| **s.73(1)** | **All taxpayers falling within "company"**, **any person having income from a long-term contract**, and **all firms, associations of persons, Hindu undivided families and artificial juridical persons having turnover exceeding Tk 10 crore OR capital exceeding Tk 5 crore** — the financial statements filed with the return **must be AUDITED AND CERTIFIED BY A REGISTERED CHARTERED ACCOUNTANT**, and the **Income Computation Sheet** prepared and certified by a registered Chartered Accountant, Cost and Management Accountant, or Income Tax Practitioner |

|---|---|---|---|
| Turnover | **১০ (দশ) কোটি টাকার অধিক** | more than **Tk 10 crore** | **> BDT 100,000,000** |
| Capital | **৫ (পাঁচ) কোটি টাকার অধিক** | more than **Tk 5 crore** | **> BDT 50,000,000** |

> ⚠️ **MATERIAL CORRECTION TO PwC.** PwC Bangladesh's *Finance Act 2026: Key Amendments* prints these as "turnover exceeding **BDT 10m** or capital exceeding **BDT 5m**" — **wrong by a factor of ten**. The enacted Bangla text of Finance Act 2026 s.59 says **১০ কোটি** and **৫ কোটি**. Verified twice: in the Finance Act 2026 itself and as substituted into ITA 2023 on bdlaws. **Use 10 crore / 5 crore.**

**The immediately preceding version** (substituted by Act 89 of 2026 s.56, in force 10 April – 30 June 2026) was structurally different and is what most secondary commentary still describes: it applied to *"any person other than a natural person, HUF and fund"* plus long-term-contract earners, with provisos excluding **firms, trusts, AOPs, foundations, societies and co-operative societies with gross receipts not exceeding Tk 5 crore** and **educational institutions engaged only in primary and pre-primary education**.

### 4.7 Auditor rotation and non-audit services

**There is NO auditor-rotation rule in the Companies Act 1994.** Rotation is imposed by sectoral regulators:

| Regulator | Rule |
|---|---|
| **BSEC — listed companies** | **Notification No. BSEC/CMRRCD/2006-158/208/Admin/81, dated 20 June 2018, *Gazette Extraordinary* 8 August 2018**, made under **s.2CC of the Securities and Exchange Ordinance 1969**. **Condition 2(2):** *"The company shall not appoint any firm of chartered accountants as its statutory auditors for a consecutive period exceeding three years."* **Condition 2(3):** the auditor or audit firm is likewise not eligible to audit for a consecutive period exceeding three years. Condition 2(1): the auditor must be on the **BSEC panel**. Conditions 2(4)–(5): report per **ISA**, ensuring compliance with the Companies Act 1994, the FRA 2015 and securities laws, and with **ISQC** and the **Code of Ethics** |
| **BSEC Corporate Governance Code 2018** | Condition 7 restricts **non-audit services** (appraisal/valuation, financial information systems design, book-keeping, broker-dealer, actuarial, internal or special audit, corporate-governance compliance certification, and anything creating a conflict of interest); condition 7(2) bars share ownership by partners, employees and their families; condition 7(3) requires the auditor's representative to attend the AGM/EGM; condition 9(1) requires an annual corporate-governance compliance certificate from a practising CA, CMA or Chartered Secretary **other than the statutory auditor** |

> ⚠️ **The three-year rotation rule is NOT in the Corporate Governance Code 2018**, contrary to much commentary. It is in the 8 August 2018 financial-reporting notification.

**Bank auditors — Bank Company Act 1991 s.39(1):** a bank company's accounts may be audited only by a person qualified as a company auditor **who is also approved and enlisted by Bangladesh Bank**. s.39(2) applies the powers, duties, liabilities and penalties of Companies Act s.213 to such an auditor.

### 4.8 Document Verification System (DVS) and the DVC

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| System | **Document Verification System (DVS)**, built by **ICAB jointly with NBR** |
| Portal | **https://dvs.icab.org.bd** |

| Where it goes | **Printed on the audit report / audited financial statements next to the auditor's signature** |
| Third-party verification | Public "Document Verification" page (OTP to an 11-digit Bangladeshi mobile); "Identify Auditor" page looks up the audit firm by company name/ID |

**Mandatory since 1 December 2020:**

| Date | Event | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **12 Nov 2020** | DVS launched; **NBR–ICAB MoU signed** the same day | — |

| **1 Dec 2020** | **Commencement** | — |

**ICAB Circular DVS/001, verbatim:** *"all audit reports **signed on Dec 01, 2020 and onwards must have the Document Verification Code (DVC) along with the auditor's signature**… **Without mentioning Document Verification Code (DVC), audited financial statement signed by any practicing member shall be considered as invalid document.**"* **Scope carve-out: management audit and internal audit are excluded.**

**What the NBR Special Order actually directs** (Letter No. 08.01.0000.030.06.009.20.178 dated 26 November 2020, signed by the Second Secretary (Tax Law-1), circulated to all Commissioners of Taxes and to the President of ICAB):

| Clause | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **(ক)** | For **all** audited financial statements filed by company-class taxpayers, the **DCT shall go to dvs.icab.org.bd and verify the DVC** |
| **(খ)** | If verification shows the statement is **not certified by a Chartered Accountant, i.e. fake**, or does not bear the signatures of an adequate number of directors, the DCT shall **reject the purportedly audited statement** and act under s.35 of the ITO 1984 |
| **(গ)** | The DCT shall record at the **head of the assessment order**: the certifying auditor's name(s); the date of certification; **the ICAB Document Verification Code**; and the name(s) of the signing director(s) |
| **(ঘ)** | Does **not** apply to audited financial statements certified **before 1 December 2020** |

> **What NBR rejects is the audited accounts, not the return.** The DCT disregards the financial statements and proceeds under the method-of-accounting power — the gateway to computing income on a basis the DCT determines.

**⭐ The DVC obligation is administrative, not statutory.** The authentic English text of the Income Tax Act 2023 contains **zero occurrences of "DVS", "DVC" or "verification code"**. The obligation rests on the NBR special order, the ICAB circular, regulator MoUs and (now) the FRC's prescribed report format. The statutory hooks are **ITA 2023 s.169(2)(b)** ("evidence of compliance with the standards prescribed by the Board, **for verification**"), ss.72–73, and the penalties in ss.273, 274 and 316. **No replacement NBR special order re-issued under the ITA 2023 was found.**

**DVC format.** Sources variously call it "18-digit", "alphanumeric" and "numerical". A published specimen settles it: **`2202241468AS244600`** — from the HSBC Bangladesh audited financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2021, signed by Gorachad Kundu FCA, Partner, A. Qasem & Co., **Enrolment No. 1468**, dated **24 February 2022**. Decoded: `220224` = signing date YYMMDD; `1468` = the partner's **ICAB enrolment number**; `AS` = an undocumented two-letter component; `244600` = sequence. **So 18 characters — 16 digits plus 2 letters.** ⚠️ This decode rests on a **single observed specimen**; ICAB publishes no format specification.

**Other regulators' use of DVS:**

> ⚠️ **A commonly mis-cited instrument:** FRC Notification **146/FRC/SS/Prokgapon/2020/71** of 21 December 2020 (gazetted 19 January 2021) concerns the **format of the auditor's signature** on PIE audit reports. **It does not mention DVS or DVC at all.** Do not cite it as a DVC instrument.

**Consequences of a missing DVC:** *professional* — the audited FS "shall be considered as invalid document" (ICAB Circular DVS/001); *tax* — the DCT rejects the audited FS, with penalties under ITA 2023 **s.273** (CA: Tk 50,000–200,000), **s.274** (taxpayer: Tk 100,000) and **s.316** (criminal: 6 months to 5 years rigorous imprisonment); *banking* — statements without a valid DVC cannot be relied on in loan files; *disciplinary* — ICAB Council suspends DVS access and DVC generation as a sanction (e.g. 16 June 2026: a member suspended from practice for 24 months with a Tk 2,000,000 penalty and retrospective DVC-generation restrictions).

**2026 DVS governance changes:** the **DVS Reinstatement Policy** (Council's 738th meeting, 2 Sep 2023), which throttled practitioners generating excessive DVCs relative to capacity, was **abolished at Council's 804th meeting on 17 February 2026** — citing conflict with **ISA 230**. The replacement **DVS Access Transitional Policy** (805th meeting, 28 February 2026, effective 1 March 2026): before mandatory QAD/TRC training, **maximum 20 DVCs, non-PIE audits only**; after training, PIE DVCs permitted **except** banks, NBFIs, insurers and listed entities, with a **maximum of 80 DVCs over the following 9 months, of which at most 15 PIE**. Separately, a **Professional Clearance (PC) Portal** launched **2 September 2026** at https://dvs.icab.org.bd/pc/login, integrated with DVS — **professional clearance is deemed granted if the consent giver does not respond within 14 days**.

**⚠️ No published ICAB fee for generating a DVC was found.** Commentary about "rising audit fees after DVS" refers to **firms' fees to clients**, not an ICAB charge — do not conflate the two.

### 4.9 ⭐ Language and currency of books of account — an honest negative finding

**No express statutory requirement that books of account be kept in Bangla or in English was found** — not in the Companies Act 1994, not in the Income Tax Act 2023 or its rules, and not in the VAT & SD Act 2012 or the VAT & SD Rules 2016. What exists is only this:

| Instrument | What it actually says |
|---|---|
| **Companies Act 1994, s.379(1)(a)** | A **foreign company** must file the certified copy of its charter/statutes/MoA & AoA and, **"if the document is not written in the English or Bangla language, a certified copy of a Bangla or English translation of it"** |
| **Companies Act 1994, s.380(2)** | **"If any document referred to in sub-section (1) is not written in the Bangla or English language, a certified Bangla or English translation shall be annexed to it"** |
| **VAT & SD Act 2012 s.107 and VAT & SD Rules 2016 rule 95** | **Contain NO language requirement.** Rule 95 requires only that records be **preserved in the registered premises on a fiscal-year basis** so they are not destroyed and can be examined easily, and that **electronic information be preserved with proper security** |
| **Income Tax Act 2023** | Full-text search found **no language requirement** for books of account. s.72(3) empowers the Board to prescribe accounting methods by rules; **NBR's published Income Tax Rules contain no rule on books of account** |
| **Bengali Language Introduction Act 1987** | Requires Bangla in courts and government, semi-government and autonomous offices. **It does not by its terms govern a private company's books of account** |

> ⚠️ **The widely repeated proposition that "the VAT rules contain a specific Bangla-or-English requirement" was NOT confirmed.** That formulation appears to originate in the **repealed VAT Rules 1991**, whose text could not be retrieved to confirm the rule number or wording. **The VAT & SD Rules 2016, in force since 1 July 2019, contain no such rule.** If a Bangla-or-English rule is to be asserted for VAT records, it needs an independently sourced citation — none could be supplied.

**Currency: likewise no express requirement** that accounts be maintained in Taka was found in any of the three statutes. The evidence is indirect: **Companies Act Schedules X and XI are denominated in টাকা throughout**, so the *filed* balance sheet, P&L and annual return are in Taka; the ITA computes tax **to the nearest Taka**; and **IAS 21** functional/presentation-currency rules apply as adopted standards.

### 4.10 Retention periods at a glance

**The income-tax retention rule is new: ITA 2023 s.72A** (*"হিসাব, দলিল, বিল-ভাউচার, ইত্যাদি সংরক্ষণের মেয়াদ ও পদ্ধতি"*), **INSERTED by the Finance Act 2026 (Act 96 of 2026) s.58, with effect from 1 July 2026**:

> The accounts, documents, supporting documents, bills-vouchers, statements, invoices or documents relating to accounts of the income of any business, class of business or other source — **(a) shall be preserved: for ALL COMPANIES, FOR THE PERIOD MENTIONED IN THE COMPANIES ACT 1994; and for ALL TAXPAYERS OTHER THAN COMPANIES, FOR 6 (SIX) YEARS; (b) may be preserved in ANALOGUE form or in DIGITAL form in accordance with the Information and Communication Technology Act 2006 (Act 39 of 2006), or in both.**

**The cross-reference resolves to twelve years** — Companies Act s.181(5).

| Regime | Period | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **Companies Act 1994 — all companies** | **12 years** (books **and vouchers**), or the whole period since incorporation if shorter | **s.181(5)** |
| **Income tax — companies** | **The Companies Act period, i.e. 12 years** | **ITA 2023 s.72A(a)** |
| **Income tax — non-companies** | **6 years** | **ITA 2023 s.72A(a)** |
| **VAT — all registered/enlisted persons** | **5 years**, and notwithstanding that, documents relating to any **unresolved proceeding until it is disposed of** | **VAT Act 2012 s.107(1), (3)** |
| **VDS withholding documents** | at least **5 years** | VDS Rules 2025 |
| **Transfer pricing records** | as prescribed by rules (the Board may prescribe the period) | **ITA 2023 s.237** |

> ⚠️ **A deliberate mismatch to manage conservatively.** The regimes prescribe 12 / 6 / 5 years, while **ITA 2023 s.212(4) proviso** allows undisclosed income, expenses or assets **older than six years** to be deemed to relate to the sixth preceding year, and s.212 proceedings are not otherwise time-limited. **The safe operating rule for a company is 12 years for everything.**

## 5. PAYROLL & EMPLOYMENT

### 5.1 What is salary income — ITA 2023 s.32

**Income from employment** includes monetary receipts, salaries and benefits received or receivable; income from employee share schemes; untaxed arrear salary; and any amount or benefit from a past or future employer.

The s.32 Explanation defines **"salary"** to include: salary, wages, remuneration; **any allowance, leave allowance, leave encashment, bonus, fees, commission, overtime**; advance salary; **gratuities, annuities, pensions**; **perquisites**; and profit in lieu of or in addition to salary.

**"Profit in lieu of salary"** includes termination compensation by whatever name, the portion excluding the employee's own PF contribution, and the fair market value of benefits from modified employment terms or from joining.

**"Perquisite"** (s.32 Explanation (c)) means any payment or benefit from employer to employee **including incentive bonus**, but excludes basic salary, arrears, advance salary, festival allowance, leave encashment and overtime, and contributions to recognised PF, approved pension, gratuity and superannuation funds. **Act 89 of 2026 added group insurance premium to the exclusion list.**

**Statutory exclusions from employment income — s.32(2)** (as amended by Act 89 of 2026 §40):

| Excluded | Provision |
|---|---|
| Amounts received for medical expenses for **heart, kidney, eye, liver and brain operations, artificial organ transplant, and cancer treatment** — for an employee who is **not a shareholder director** | s.32(2)(a) as substituted |
| **Conveyance, travelling and daily allowance** wholly and exclusively incurred in performing the duties of employment | s.32(2)(b) |
| **Group insurance premium** paid by the company to an insurer on an employee's behalf | new s.32(2)(c) |
| Medical expenses **reimbursed under a group insurance policy** | FA 2026 change (PwC p.7) |
| Expenses incurred by an employee for official work, where necessary and advantageous to the employer | Sixth Schedule Part 1, para 14 |

**Quick taxability reference** (ACE Advisory, *Bangladesh Tax Insights 2026-2027*, 10th ed. — https://aceadvisory.biz/resource/publications/bangladesh-tax-insights-2025-2026):

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Basic salary, festival bonus, performance bonus, dearness allowance, leave encashment, leave fare assistance | Fully taxable |
| **Employer's contribution to recognised PF** | **Fully taxable** in the employee's hands |
| Interest income from recognised PF | Exempt (6th Sch Pt 1 para 6) |
| Approved gratuity fund | Exempt up to **Tk 25,000,000** |
| Group insurance premium and insurer-reimbursed medical | Exempt |
| **Workers' Profit Participation Fund (WPPF)** | **Fully taxable — no exemption limit** |
| Telephone / mobile allowance, reimbursements | Tax free so far as for official use |
| Other allowances | Fully taxable |

**Employee share schemes — s.34:** taxable on receipt at fair market value less acquisition cost. Act 89 of 2026 extended s.34 to the **"right to acquire shares"**, not just to shares.

### 5.2 ⭐ The consolidated salary exemption — the single cap

> **Sixth Schedule, Part 1, paragraph (27):**
> **One-third of income computed under "income from employment", OR Tk 500,000 — whichever is LOWER — is excluded from total income.**

**The cap is Tk 500,000, not Tk 450,000.** The change was made by **Act 89 of 2026 §135(ক)(৭)**, which replaced "৪ (চার) লক্ষ ৫০ (পঞ্চাশ) হাজার" with "৫ (পাঁচ) লক্ষ". Confirmed by four independent sources:

### 5.4 Perquisites, company car and accommodation

> ⚠️ **KPMG's *Bangladesh Tax 2026* has a typo in this table** — it prints overlapping "1500 cc to 2500 cc" and "2000 cc to 2500 cc" bands. The correct second band is **1,500–2,000 cc**.

**Employer-side cap — s.55(d):** perquisites payable to an employee are limited to **Tk 2,500,000 per year**; any excess is **disallowed in the company's tax assessment**. (Tk 1,000,000 originally → Tk 2,000,000 by Act 89 of 2026 §50(খ) → **Tk 2,500,000** by the Finance Act 2026.) The cap does **not** apply where perquisites were paid under a Government-gazetted Wage Board recommendation.

**Also disallowed for the employer — s.55(k):** any payment under the head *income from employment* made **by any means other than bank transfer**.

### 5.5 Employer's obligation to deduct tax from salary

| Item | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Deduction** | **s.86(1)** — the person responsible for payment under "income from employment" shall, **at the time of making payment**, deduct tax at **the average of the rates applicable to the estimated total income** of the payee under that head |
| Government DDOs | s.86(3) — deduct at the average rate on estimated total income where annual taxable salary exceeds the taxable limit |
| Mid-year adjustment | s.86(4) — surplus or deficit adjusted; Act 89 of 2026 §61 changed "previous" to **"current income year"** |
| Lower / nil deduction certificate | s.86(5) — the DCT may certify nil or lower deduction for the remainder of the income year, on the employee's application and evidence |
| Deposit to Government | **s.146** — within the prescribed time (see 2.16) |
| Certificate to the employee | **s.145** — Schedule-1 of SRO 273, within 2 weeks of the month following deduction |

**Consequences of under-deduction — s.143:** the employer is deemed an **assessee-in-default** and is liable for the tax not deducted, plus **2% per month capped at 24 months** (s.143(3)–(4)); the organisation **and** the individuals who approved the payment are **jointly and severally liable** (s.143(6)–(7)); issuing a deduction certificate without actual deduction makes the issuer **personally liable** (s.144). The additional-tax charge on the payer is **150% of the tax defaulted** under the rebuilt s.56 — see 2.18, where NBR's own worked example confirms the arithmetic.

**PSR uplift:** if a payee liable to furnish a Proof of Submission of Return fails to do so, the withholding rate is **50% higher** (s.142(1)).

### 5.6 ⭐ The employer's salary statement obligation — a structural change

**The Income Tax Act 2023 does NOT carry forward the old ITO 1984 s.108 ("Statement regarding payment of salary") or s.108A ("Information regarding filing of return by employees") as standalone sections.** The full section index of the Act was checked and no equivalent exists. **The obligation is now discharged inside the quarterly withholding return under s.177** (see 2.17 for filers and deadlines: 25 October, 25 January, 25 April, 25 July; no extension available).

**Annual salary statements filed with the quarterly return — ⚠️ lower confidence, see UNVERIFIED:**

| Filing | Deadline | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Statement of yearly salary payments (employee-wise salary paid and TDS deducted) | **25 October**, with the Jul–Sep quarterly withholding return | Schedule "Ga"/"Ca" of the s.177 return |
| Employee tax statement | **25 April**, with the Jan–Mar quarterly return | Schedule "Cha" |

The s.177 return format comprises four parts plus **two schedules**, which is consistent with two annual statements — but the schedule letters and content could not be verified against the Rules text. NBR's public forms portal still serves the obsolete ITO 1984 s.75A form.

**Penalties:** failure to file the s.177 return — higher of **10% of tax on last assessed income** or **Tk 5,000**, plus **Tk 1,000 per month** of continuing default (s.266(2)(a)). Failure to furnish the s.145 certificate — up to **Tk 5,000** plus **Tk 1,000 per month** (s.266(2)(b)).

### 5.7 ⭐ What replaced the old "list of employees who filed returns"

Under **s.264(3)** as wholly substituted by **Act 89 of 2026 §122**, PSR must be furnished by:

- **Item 24** — any person **receiving salary and allowances in a management, administrative or production-supervisory position**;
- **Item 25** — **public servants of grade 10 or above** receiving salary and allowances.

And **s.55(o)** as amended **disallows, as a business expense, any sum paid to a person liable to furnish PSR under clauses 24, 26, 27, 33, 36 and 37 where that person fails to furnish PSR at the time of payment.** **Clause 24 is in that list** — so **salary paid to a managerial, administrative or supervisory employee who has not produced a PSR is disallowed in the employer's assessment.** This is a materially sharper sanction than the old s.108A reporting duty.

### 5.8 Provident fund — Second Schedule, Part 3

**Recognition conditions:**

| Item | Rule | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Non-applicability | Part 3 does not apply to a PF governed by the **Provident Funds Act 1925** | 2nd Sch Pt 3 para 1 |
| Employees' location | All employees must be employed in Bangladesh or by an employer whose principal place of business is in Bangladesh; the **Commissioner** may recognise a fund of a foreign-based employer where **≤10%** of employees are outside Bangladesh | para 2(a) |
| Employee contribution | A **definite proportion of salary**, deducted at each periodical payment and credited to the employee's individual account | para 2(b) |
| **Employer contribution cap** | **Shall not exceed the employee's own contribution** in that year, credited at intervals not exceeding one year | para 2(c) |
| Trust | Vested in **two or more trustees** under an irrevocable trust | para 2(e) |
| Employer recovery | Employer may not recover any sum from the fund, save on dismissal for misconduct or voluntary resignation otherwise than on specified grounds | para 2(f) |
| Application | By the trustees in writing to the **Commissioner of Taxes**, with the trust deed and fund rules | ACE §2.33 |
| Timing | Recognition takes effect **within 60 days** of application on approval, and the recognition date must fall **on or before the last day of the same fiscal year** | ACE §2.33 |

**Tax treatment:**

| Party / item | Treatment | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Employer's contribution — deductibility | **Deductible** from income, profits and gains | 2nd Sch Pt 3 para 5(1) |
| Employer's contribution — employee's hands | **Fully taxable** as salary | s.32 |
| Employee's and employer's contribution | Eligible investment for the **investment tax rebate** | 6th Sch Pt 3 |
| Contributions received by the fund | Exempt in the fund's hands | 6th Sch Pt 1 para 6(a) |
| Income distributed to beneficiaries already taxed in the fund's hands | Exempt | 6th Sch Pt 1 para 6(b) |
| **Interest / other income from the fund** | Exempt up to **A**, where A < (B × 33%); the excess **A − (B × 33%)** is included in income. A = amount received from the fund in the income year; B = income from employment excluding that amount | **2nd Sch Pt 3 para 5(2)** |
| **Accumulated balance on withdrawal** | **Exempt** if the employee has rendered **continuous service with the employer for not less than 5 years**. The Commissioner may allow the exemption for shorter service where employment ended through **ill health, contraction or discontinuance of the employer's business, or other cause beyond the employee's control** | **2nd Sch Pt 3 para 6(1)** |
| If the 5-year condition fails | The DCT recomputes tax for each year as if the fund had never been recognised; the excess is payable in addition to the tax for the year of payment | para 6(2) |
| TDS on payment of accumulated balance | Treated as **salary**, deducted at **average rates** | para 6(4) |
| **Unrecognised PF** | Employer contributions are **NOT allowable** (s.55(u): "any contribution to any fund for which there is provision of approval under this Act but no such approval is obtained"); **no investment rebate** for employees | s.55(u) |

**Government vs non-government:**

| Fund type | Treatment | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| PF under the **Provident Funds Act 1925** (GPF and equivalents) | **All income accruing to or derived by the fund is exempt** | 6th Sch Pt 1 **para 7** |
| Recognised PF, approved superannuation, pension or provident fund (non-government) | Contributions received, and taxed income distributed to beneficiaries, exempt | 6th Sch Pt 1 **para 6** |
| Voluntary retirement payments to employees of government organisations, local authorities and autonomous/semi-autonomous bodies under a Government-approved scheme | Exempt | 6th Sch Pt 1 **para 8** |

An establishment that constitutes a PF under Labour Act s.264 is **deemed a government institution for the purposes of the Provident Funds Act 1925** (Labour Act s.264(17)).

**The 2023 "PF taxation" scare — resolved.** ICAB and others warned in 2023 that the ITA 2023 had made private-sector PF, gratuity and WPPF fund income taxable at 27.5% for the first time (The Daily Star, 20 Sept 2023, https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/provident-funds-pay-275-tax-3423051). ACE Advisory's 2026-27 edition now states plainly that **"under the ITA 2023 funds are not required to submit annual income tax returns"**, citing s.166(2); this was independently confirmed from the bdlaws text of Act 89 of 2026 §95(খ), whose substituted **s.166(2)(ঞ)** exempts "**তহবিল**" (funds) from mandatory return filing.

**Finance Act 2026 changes:** **recognition of the PF is now REQUIRED for the investment tax rebate** on PF contributions, and **premature withdrawal of rebate-earning investments attracts pro-rated additional tax**.

**⭐ When a PF becomes mandatory — changed by the Labour (Amendment) Act 2026.** **Labour Act 2006 s.264(10)**, as substituted by the **Bangladesh Labour (Amendment) Act 2026 (Act No. 43 of 2026) §56(খ)** (read from http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-952/section-26316.html):

> In establishments with **not less than 100 permanent workers**, the owner **shall be bound** to constitute a provident fund if **not less than two-thirds** of the workers demand it by written application.

- **s.264(9):** every permanent worker, after **completing one year of service**, contributes monthly **not less than 7% and not more than 8% of monthly basic wages** (unless otherwise agreed); **the employer contributes at the same rate**.
- **s.264(11):** where demanded, the owner must frame rules within **six months** of the application and start the fund before that period expires.
- **s.264(15):** the fund's audited accounts and audit report must be sent to the **Director of Labour within one month** of submission of the audit report.

> ⚠️ **PwC's Worldwide Tax Summaries is out of date here** — it states "at least three-fourths of the total number of workers" and omits the 100-employee threshold. The primary text says **two-thirds** and **≥100 permanent workers**.

### 5.9 Gratuity

| Source of gratuity | Exemption | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **Government Gratuity Fund** | Income up to **Tk 25,000,000** (Tk 2 crore 50 lakh) | 6th Sch Pt 1 **para 5** |
| **Any NBR-approved gratuity fund** | Amount received "shall not exceed the limit of Taka 2 crore and 50 lakh" | 6th Sch Pt 1 **para 6, proviso** |
| **Unapproved / internal gratuity fund** | Employer contributions are **not allowable expenditure** | s.55(u) |

The Tk 25,000,000 figure is confirmed by NBR's authentic English text, ACE Advisory and KPMG.

**Approved gratuity fund — Second Schedule Part 2:**
- **Para 1 — conditions:** an irrevocable trust connected with a trade or undertaking in Bangladesh; **not less than 90%** of employees employed in Bangladesh; sole purpose is gratuity on retirement, after a specified age, on incapacity, on termination after a minimum service period, or to widows, children or dependants on death; **the employer must be a contributor**; all benefits payable only in Bangladesh.
- **Para 2 — application** in writing by a trustee to the Commissioner of Taxes with the gratuity deed and rules; ACE reports approval may be granted **within 180 days** of receipt.
- **Para 4:** any sum paid by an employer as **contribution to an approved gratuity fund is deducted** in computing income, profits and gains.
- **Para 5:** if a contribution (with interest) is repaid to the employer, that refund is **income of the employer** in the year of receipt.

**Labour Act entitlement — s.2(10)** (verbatim from http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-952/section-29070.html):

> "গ্রাচুইটি" means, for each **completed year of service or service in excess of six months**, wages of **not less than 30 days** at the worker's **last drawn wage rate**; or, where the service **exceeds 10 years**, **45 days'** wages at the last drawn wage rate — payable on termination of service.

**Gratuity floors across the Labour Act — the "or gratuity, whichever is higher" rule.** The Act does not require a *funded* scheme, but the **entitlement itself is compulsory** because it appears as a floor in each separation provision:

| Provision | Entitlement |
|---|---|
| **s.2(10)** definition | ≥30 days' wages at the last drawn rate per completed year (or service exceeding six months); **45 days' wages per year where service exceeds 10 years** |
| **s.20(2)(c)** retrenchment | ≥1 year's service: **30 days' wages per year, or gratuity, whichever is higher** (plus 15 days extra if retrenched under s.16(7)) |
| **s.22(2)** discharge (incapacity) | 30 days per year, or gratuity, whichever is higher |
| **s.23(3)** dismissal for misconduct | **15 days' wages per year**; nil for theft, fraud, riot etc. (s.23(4)) |
| **s.26(4)** termination by employer | 30 days per year, or gratuity, whichever is higher; notice 120 / 60 / 30 / 14 days |
| **s.27(4) resignation — ⭐ REWRITTEN by the Labour (Amendment) Act 2026 §13** | **≤3 years: 7 days' wages per year; >3 to <10 years: 15 days per year; ≥10 years: 30 days per year or gratuity, whichever is higher.** *(The old rule gave nothing below 5 years.)* |
| **s.30** | **All dues are payable within 30 WORKING DAYS of cessation of employment** |

**Retirement — s.28:** a worker retires normally on completing **60 years** of age (raised from 57 by the Labour (Amendment) Act 2010 §2). The date of birth in the service book is proof of age. A retired worker may be re-engaged on contract.

**Note on the amending instrument:** the 2026 labour changes were first made by the **Bangladesh Labour (Amendment) Ordinance 2025 (Ordinance 65 of 2025, 17 November 2025)**, which was then repealed and re-enacted as the **Bangladesh Labour (Amendment) Act 2026 (Act 43 of 2026)**.

### 5.10 ⭐ Workers' Profit Participation Fund (WPPF)

**Labour Act 2006, Chapter XV — all figures read from the primary text on bdlaws.**

| Item | Rule | Provision |

| **Which companies are covered** | A company or establishment meeting **either**: (a) **paid-up capital of not less than Tk 10,000,000 (Tk 1 crore)** on the last day of an accounting year; **or** (b) **value of fixed assets of not less than Tk 20,000,000 (Tk 2 crore)** on the last day of an accounting year | **s.232(1)**, substituted by the Labour (Amendment) Act 2013 §63 — http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-952/section-26285.html |

| Use of the fund | **Two-thirds distributed in cash equally each year; one-third invested** | **s.242** |

### 5.11 Superannuation, pension funds and the Universal Pension Scheme

**Approved superannuation or pension fund — Second Schedule Part 1:** conditions for approval (para 1); procedure (para 2, application by trustees to the Commissioner); withdrawal of approval (para 3); income and subscriptions (para 4); trustees' liabilities on cessation (para 5); particulars to be furnished (para 6). **Employer contributions** are deductible and excluded from "perquisite" (s.32 Explanation (c)(ii)); **annual contribution to an approved superannuation fund qualifies for the investment rebate with no limit**. **Any pension due to or received from the Government or an approved Pension Fund is exempt** (6th Sch Pt 1 **para 4**).

**⭐ Universal Pension Scheme (সর্বজনীন পেনশন) — is it mandatory for private employers in 2026? NO — but it is now an express statutory ALTERNATIVE to the mandatory provident fund.**

**Labour Act 2006 s.264**, marginal note substituted by the **Bangladesh Labour (Amendment) Act 2026 (Act No. 43 of 2026) §56(ক)** to read: *"Provident fund **or participation in the Universal Pension Scheme** for workers of private sector establishments"* (http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-952/section-26316.html).

**s.264(10)** as substituted by the same Act §56(খ):

> In establishments with **≥100 permanent workers**, the owner is bound to constitute a provident fund if **≥two-thirds** of workers demand it in writing — **BUT the owner is EXEMPTED from that obligation if he ensures institutional participation, for those employees who express written interest, in the Universal Pension Scheme "প্রগতি" (Progoti) introduced by the National Pension Authority.**
> **Proviso:** in that scheme the **owner pays 50% of the contribution and the worker 50%**.
> **Further proviso:** if a worker expresses written unwillingness to join, the owner is **not bound** to pay his 50%.

So participation is **not compulsory for private employers generally**. It becomes relevant once the ≥100-worker / two-thirds-demand trigger for a mandatory PF is met, at which point the employer may elect Progoti instead — and if he does, a **50/50 employer/employee split** applies for consenting workers.

**Tax treatment** (from the bdlaws text of Act 89 of 2026 §135(ক)(১০) and (১১)):

| Provision | Effect |
|---|---|
| **6th Sch Pt 1 para (34)** — substituted: "জাতীয় পেনশন কর্তৃপক্ষের সর্বজনীন পেনশন স্কিম হইতে প্রাপ্ত সুবিধাভোগীর কোনো আয়" | **Any income of a beneficiary from the National Pension Authority's Universal Pension Scheme is exempt** — broadened from the narrower original (income arising as *pension*) to cover **any** beneficiary income |
| **6th Sch Pt 1 para (34ক)** — new: "জাতীয় পেনশন কর্তৃপক্ষের আয়" | **The income of the National Pension Authority itself is exempt** |
| Contributions | **Subscription to the Universal Pension Scheme is an eligible investment for the tax rebate with NO limit** |

## B. Bill-versus-Act confusion in the public record (resolved, but pervasive)

**B1. The tax-free thresholds.** Budget-day reporting of 11 June 2026 — including *bssnews.net* and *Dhaka Tribune* — states a general threshold of **Tk 375,000**, Tk 425,000 for women and seniors, Tk 500,000 for persons with disability and third gender, and Tk 525,000 for freedom fighters. **These are the Finance *Bill* figures.** Parliament raised each band by one step before passing the Act. Verified by comparing KPMG's *Salient features of Finance Bill 2026* (which shows 375,000 / 400,000 / 450,000 across the three periods) against all three post-enactment summaries (which show 400,000 / 450,000 / 500,000). *The Daily Star* headline "Finance Bill passed, tax-free income threshold set at Tk 4 lakh for FY2026-27" confirms the direction. **Use the enacted figures.** Many secondary sites still publish the Bill figures.

**B2. Minimum tax.** Sources still widely publish the location-based scale (Tk 5,000 Dhaka/Chattogram city corporations, Tk 4,000 other city corporations, Tk 3,000 elsewhere). That scale is **abolished** from AY 2026-27; the figure is a flat **Tk 5,000** (Tk 1,000 for new taxpayers). Confirmed by KPMG p.47 and TNP p.25.

## C. Income tax — items I could not confirm, or where sources disagree

**C1. The corporate "cashless" condition — do the Tk 500,000 / Tk 3,600,000 de-minimis thresholds survive?** Some secondary commentary still states that only single transactions above Tk 500,000 or annual aggregates above Tk 3,600,000 must be routed through banking channels. But PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries (reviewed 31 July 2026) states the conditions without any threshold — "All transactions to be undertaken through bank transfer" and "All income is received through bank transfer" — and KPMG flags the move to "**all** transactions" as an FA 2026 change. **I could not read the enacted condition.** Treat it as an unqualified 100% requirement and verify before relying on any de-minimis. This is commercially material: it decides a 2.5-percentage-point rate difference.

**C2. Merchant banks at 27.5% and One Person Companies at 27.5%.** Reported by Tuhin & Partners and corroborated by Jural Acuity. **Neither PwC nor KPMG lists these categories separately**, so I have two sources of similar type rather than genuinely independent confirmation. The OPC change is significant — from 22.5%/20% to 27.5% regardless of bank-transfer compliance.

**C3. "Funds" taxed at 15%.** Single source (TNP p.24). Not corroborated.

**C4. Textile sector.** The 15% reduced rate under SRO 159/2022 expired 30 June 2025. Trade press (Bangladesh Textile Journal, 19 May 2025; The Business Standard) reports withdrawal with no renewal, putting textile firms on 27.5%/22.5%. **No NBR notification confirming non-renewal was located**, and none of the three FA 2026 summaries mentions textiles either way. Treat the lapse as probable but unconfirmed.

**C5. Solar power exemption end date — sources conflict.** PwC says full income tax exemption "up to 30 June 2035". KPMG says the exemption runs "between 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2036". TNP says "1 July 2026 to 30 June 2035" under SRO 211-AIN/Income Tax-2/2026. **Two of three say 2035; KPMG says 2036.** Unresolved.

**C6. Edible oil exemption tenure — sources conflict.** PwC: "100%, 50%, and 25% over 10 years". TNP: 100% years 1–5, 50% years 6–8, 25% years 9–10. KPMG: 100% first five years, 50% next three, 25% remaining two — and says the exemption applies "between 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2036" while TNP implies a ten-year run from commencement. The **tranche pattern is agreed (5/3/2)**; the outer date is not.

**C7. The Sixth Schedule paragraph number for the salary allowance exemption** was not identified. The cap itself (one-third of employment income or Tk 500,000, whichever is lower) comes from KPMG p.45.

**C8. Advance tax threshold — "income" or "tax liability"?** PwC describes the raised threshold as "BDT1m of the **estimated tax liability**". KPMG describes it as last assessed **income** exceeding Tk 1,000,000, with Tk 600,000 of estimated income for new taxpayers. PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries agrees with KPMG ("taxpayers whose prior-year income exceeded BDT 1 million"). **Two sources to one favour "income"**, and that also matches the pre-existing structure of s.154. PwC's phrasing is probably loose, but I could not verify the section.

**C9. The entity late-filing minimum — Tk 25,000 or Tk 3,000?** PwC's entity table gives "Additional tax of 2% (min BDT25,000)", which looked like a transcription error from the individual table (min Tk 3,000). **It is not an error:** TNP and KPMG both independently give Tk 25,000 for entities. Resolved in favour of **Tk 25,000**, but noted because the asymmetry with the individual figure is surprising.

**C10. Does the statutory term "Tax Day" still exist?** A search result indicates the phrase was replaced via **s.2(80Ka)** by the **Finance Ordinance 2025** — i.e. before the Finance Act 2026 — and the Act now works off a "due date for submission of return". The Finance Act 2026 separately introduces a ceremonial "**National Tax Day**" at s.2(22), which is an awareness observance, not a deadline. **I could not verify the drafting.** What is well established is the operative effect: individuals face no additional tax if they file by **31 December**, and companies by the **15th day of the ninth month following the income year end** (15 March for a June year-end).

**C11. The individual filing "deadline" is an inference.** No source states a single statutory individual due date. The 31 December figure is derived from the fact that the 1 October – 31 December window carries neither rebate nor additional tax, corroborated by NBR's own press statement that "if the return is submitted after December, additional tax will have to be paid". Also unverified: the special rule PwC WWTS mentions that **for first-time filers the deadline is 30 June** following the tax year end.

**C12. The PSR service list is out of date.** The list reproduced at §1.13 is the **Finance Act 2024** version (43 statutory items, though the source page numbers 45). TNP records that the Finance Act 2026 "rationalised" the ambit of s.264 and added new triggers. **I could not obtain the s.264 list as currently amended.** Do not treat the list as complete or current.

**C13. Whether any SRO reduces the s.123 export withholding rate below the statutory 1%** was not exhaustively checked against NBR's SRO index.

## D. Withholding tax — the dominant error in circulation

**D1. SRO 210 versus SRO 273 — discard any chart citing SRO 210.** Two different SROs issued in 2026 carry the **identical title** উৎসে কর বিধিমালা, ২০২৬. SRO 210-Ain/Aykar-1/2026 of 8 June 2026 was **expressly repealed** by Rule 14 of SRO 273-Ain/Aykar-5/2026 of 5 July 2026. Nearly every secondary FY2026-27 TDS chart online (taxvatpoint.com, caripon.com, taxpertbd.com, SlideShare and Facebook decks) reproduces the repealed SRO 210. The divergence is material: under SRO 210 the catering / PR / event management / creative media group was **4% on gross**; under the operative SRO 273 the catering group is **2% on total bill** and creative media sits at **10% on commission or 1% on gross bill, higher of**.

**D2. A widely repeated citation error:** "SRO No. 273-Law/Income Tax-5/**2028**, dated **30 June 2026**". Both elements are wrong — the instrument is ২৭৩-আইন/আয়কর-৫/**২০২৬**, gazetted **5 July 2026**.

**D3. NBR's own download page mislabels the SRO 273 PDF** as "The Withholding Tax Rules, 2026 (**Amendment**)". It is a complete standalone re-enactment.

**D4. No professional-firm publication reflecting SRO 273 was found.** PwC's *Finance Act 2026: Key Amendments* corroborates the **Act-level** changes but predates and does not cover SRO 273. The rate tables in §§2.1, 2.3 and 2.13 therefore rest entirely on the two primary renderings (the gazette and NBR's Paripatra), which agree with each other on every serial, plus an independent reading of the gazette page images. **There is no third-party professional cross-check on those three tables.**

**D5. Do the two s.142 uplifts stack, and is there a maximum?** **Genuinely unresolved.** Both s.142(1) (no PSR) and s.142(2) (payment not by bank transfer) independently say the rate is "50% higher than the applicable rate", neither cross-refers to the other, and **neither SRO 273 nor the Paripatra 2026-27 contains any worked example applying either uplift, let alone both**. There is **no stated maximum** anywhere. Seek a ruling before applying a combined rate.

**D6. Not transcribed:** Rule 8 (import-stage collection under s.120) HS-code schedules, and Rules 6 and 7 (property transfer under s.125 and developers under s.126), which are mouza-by-mouza tables running to dozens of pages. Their existence and statutory ceilings were confirmed; the rates were not extracted.

**D7. Whether the original 2023 enactment of s.119 contained the non-resident table in the section itself** is an inference from the absence of an amendment footnote, not a verified fact. It does not affect current rates, which are set by Rule 5.

**D8. Serial 4 of the Rule 3 goods list** contains a token rendering as "আয়োডিন" between "ময়দা" and "লবণ", probably "আয়োডিনযুক্ত লবণ" (iodised salt) split across a line break. Immaterial to rates, but confirm against a clean copy before coding the goods list.

**D9. KPMG's June deposit deadline summary is a simplification.** KPMG renders the rule as "21–29 June → following day; 30 June → same date". The actual Rule 9 turns on the **last *working* day of June** (শেষ কর্মদিবস), which differs from 30 June whenever that date falls on a weekend or public holiday.

## E. VAT — items not confirmed, or where sources disagree

**E1. The Third Schedule — rate skeleton obtained, GOODS DESCRIPTIONS NOT.** The official consolidated Schedules were located as a separate 86-page PDF linked from the act-1106 page, generated **27 August 2026** with amendment footnotes: http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/upload/act/2026-08-27-12-19-29-মূল্য-সংযোজন-কর-ও-সম্পূরক-শুল্ক-আইন,-২০১২-(1st,-2nd,-3rd-schedule).pdf — but **its text layer uses a broken legacy Bangla mapping: service codes, rate figures and footnotes are readable; goods descriptions largely are not.** So the Table 1–4 structure, the service codes and rates, the deletion footnotes and the Finance Act 2026 amendments (read separately from the Act 96 gazette in clean Unicode) are **verified**; **the item-by-item goods lists are NOT**, and the illustrative goods examples in §3.1 come from a secondary FY2025-26 chart. Do not rely on them without checking the current Schedule.

**E1a. Paragraph 3 of the Third Schedule — the local trader rate appears to have risen from 5% to 7.5%, but the full paragraph could not be read.** Footnote 121 confirms paragraph (3) was substituted by **Act 77 of 2026 s.4(ছ)**, and the readable fragments show "7." where the old text had "5". **The 7.5% / 4.5% / 2.4% / 2% line-by-line values are therefore NOT certified from primary text.**

**E1b. ⭐ Several headline "Third Schedule" rates now rest on SROs, not the Schedule.** The Schedule shows S001 (hotel/restaurant), S014 (indenting), S022 and S078 as **deleted** in January 2025, yet restaurant 5%, non-AC hotel 10%, motor garage 10% and sweetmeat 10% are genuinely current and are the rates the VDS SRO uses. The reconciliation is that **SRO 39-Ain/2025/275-Mushak of 22 January 2025 re-instated them by notification**, and **SRO 40-Ain/2025/276-Mushak of the same date restored medicines to 2.4%**. ⚠️ **Those two SROs' texts were NOT opened** — their effect was inferred from a secondary reading plus the Schedule's deletion footnotes. **Anyone citing the Schedule alone will get these rates wrong.**

**E2. RESOLVED — the VDS table in §3.10 was obtained in full from the gazette.** SRO 182-Ain/2025/310-Mushak (27 May 2025, https://nbr.gov.bd/uploads/sros/VATSRO-1822.pdf) and its amending SRO 140-Ain/2026/345-Mushak (7 June 2026, https://nbr.gov.bd/uploads/sros/VAT_SRO-140.pdf) were both read. All 45 serials are primary-verified. Two consequences worth restating: **there is no S007.20 online-advertisement code at 5% VDS in any gazette** (the SRO carries a single S007.00 advertising agency entry at 15%), and **S037.00 Procurement Provider is 10%**.

**E3. RESOLVED — "SRO 258-Law/2026/358-VAT" DOES NOT EXIST.** That citation circulates on practitioner blogs and is wrong. NBR's VAT-SRO index tops out at SRO 148-Ain/2026/353-Mushak (11 June 2026), and every probe of the 149–266 range returns 404. **The real FY2026-27 VDS amendment is SRO 140-Ain/2026/345-Mushak of 7 June 2026.**

**E3a. But a post-budget SRO wave exists that NBR has not uploaded.** PwC cites **SRO 255-Ain/2026/355-Mushak of 30 June 2026**, which is inside the un-served range. **Four announced VAT measures are therefore not traceable to any gazette**: the double-cabin pickup / microbus cut from 15% to 5%; the BTRC revenue-sharing VDS exemption; the **digital-advertising 5% rate and its S007.20 code**; and the input-output-coefficient relaxation. Treat all four as announced but unverified.

**E4. RESOLVED — S037.00 Procurement Provider is 10%.** The gazette reads "২২. S০৩৭.০০ যোগানদার (Procurement Provider) ১০%". The "5%" figure in circulation is wrong.

**E4a. ⚠️ ICAB's "S004.00 remains 7.5% for contracts signed before 30 June 2025" is NOT in SRO 182.** The full SRO text was searched and no such proviso exists. Treat it as a practice position, not the SRO.

**E4b. ⚠️ PwC is wrong on VAT on rent.** PwC states that VAT on premises rent is "reported as an increasing adjustment in the VAT return". The **substituted rule 3(5)** of the VDS Rules says **every tenant, registered or not, now pays 15% by A-Challan or e-payment** and shows the payment evidence in the **treasury-deposit section** of the return — **the registered-tenant increasing-adjustment route is gone.**

**E4c. ⚠️ PwC is wrong on the diamond fixed VAT.** PwC prints Tk 2,500 **per bhori**; the Finance Act 2026 gazette says **প্রতি গ্রাম — per GRAM**. A bhori is about 11.66 grams, so PwC's version understates the charge roughly twelve-fold.

**E5. Rule numbers for Mushak 4.3, 4.3.1, 6.2.1 and 9.1.1 are not established.** The NBR-hosted Rules PDF is the **original 3 November 2016 gazette** and does not contain Mushak 4.3 or 6.2.1 — both were added by later amending SROs that could not be located. Rule numbers for the two new FY2026-27 forms (4.3.1, 9.1.1) are unknown.

**E6. The commonly cited "SRO 186-Ain/2016" for the VAT Rules appears to be a wrong citation.** The Rules 2016 are **SRO No. 333-Ain/2016/1-Mushak dated 3 November 2016**, verified from the gazette PDF.

**E7. The former content of s.46(2)(gha)**, deleted by FA 2026, was not read — only the deletion marker.

**E8. NBR's own VAT FAQ page is badly out of date and must NOT be used.** https://nbr.gov.bd/faq/vat-faq/eng still states a Tk 8,000,000 registration threshold, Tk 3,000,000 enlistment threshold, 3% turnover tax, monthly returns by the 15th, and quarterly Mushak 9.2 — all pre-2019 figures. This stale official page is the likely origin of the "Tk 80 lakh" figure circulating in secondary write-ups.

**E9. Thresholds — resolved, but almost universally misreported.** Most secondary sources, including 2025–26 practitioner blogs, still say Tk 50 lakh enlistment / Tk 3 crore registration. The statute is unambiguous: **Tk 30 lakh enlistment / Tk 50 lakh registration** since **9 January 2025** (s.2(48) and s.2(57), substituted by Act 77 of 2026). Resolved from the bdlaws amendment footnotes, corroborated by DFDL and PwC.

**E10. ITC time limit — resolved against near-universal published guidance.** Almost every published source, including the ICAB CA manual, says **four** tax periods. The current statute says **six** (s.46(1)(ga), amended by Act 89 of 2026 s.5(a)(i)). Confidence in the statute is high, but practitioners will encounter the older figure everywhere.

**E11. Late filing fee — resolved.** Commonly cited as Tk 10,000. The statute says **Tk 2,000** (s.85(1)(cha)), reduced from Tk 5,000. The Tk 10,000 figure applies to *other* items in the s.85 table, not to late filing.

**E12. Turnover-tax tax period — resolved by inference, not by an explicit source.** The Finance Bill 2026 proposed a **4-month** cycle; PwC's post-enactment note says the period is aligned to a **Gregorian calendar month**. The enacted s.2(30) defines a tax period as one calendar month with no carve-out, and s.64 requires filing every three tax periods — so PwC appears correct and the 4-month proposal was not enacted. Stated as an inference.

**E13. Possible conflict on VAT return extensions.** KPMG states that a one-month filing extension is available "by paying delay interest of 1% per month on the net VAT payable after obtaining approval from the VAT authority (interest may be waived at the discretion of VAT authority)". **s.65 does not provide for waiver** — it says permission does not alter the liability to pay interest. A waiver power does exist, but in **s.64(3)**, where it belongs to the **Board** (not the local VAT authority) and extends the deadline "without interest or penalty". The two provisions may have been conflated.

**E14. Not verified in a primary source:** the double-cabin pickup / microbus 15%→5% change, full VAT exemption for fish supplies, and the BTRC revenue-sharing VDS exemption. These come from search summaries and the PwC document respectively.

**E15. Bangla form names** in the Mushak table are renderings from decoded rule headings and the ICAB manual; the official Bangla titles should be checked against the form schedule in the amended Rules.

## F. Payroll and employment — items not confirmed, or where sources disagree

**F1. The annual salary statement is the weakest item in section 5.** The 25 October "Schedule Ga/Ca" statement of yearly salary payments and the 25 April "Schedule Cha" employee tax statement come from **one compliance-calendar site** (Khan Akber & Co., https://khanakber.com/bangladesh-annual-compliance-calendar/) plus a corroborating summary of similar type. **They were NOT verified against the TDS Rules 2026 text or against any NBR form.** ACE Advisory's own section on the quarterly return does **not** mention a separate salary statement, and ACE's on-going-obligations page still lists a legacy "**31 August**" annual statement, which conflicts. NBR's public forms portal still serves the obsolete ITO 1984 s.75A form. **What IS confirmed:** the Income Tax Act 2023 carries no standalone equivalent of ITO 1984 ss.108/108A, and the s.177 return format comprises four parts plus **two** schedules — which is consistent with two annual statements, but does not prove their content or deadlines.

**F2. The government-employee SRO number.** SRO No. 225-Law/Income Tax-7/2023 dated 13 July 2023 comes from ACE Advisory (reliable) plus a corporate social-media post. New Age and Prothom Alo corroborate the **substance** (a July 2023 notification; only basic salary, festival allowance and bonus taxable) but **neither prints the SRO number**. New Age's rendering of "house rent allowance (up to Tk 4,50,000 or one-third of rent, whichever is lower)" appears garbled and should not be relied on.

**F3. The 27.5% vs 15% fund-level tax question is unresolved.** Two Daily Star pieces give different rates for tax on the investment income of provident, gratuity and WPPF funds. **No primary charging section was located**, and ACE Advisory states that funds need not file returns at all (s.166(2)(ঞ), which was verified). **Get a formal opinion before assuming a fund-level rate.**

**F4. Effective date of the s.88 / s.163 rewrite.** Act 89 of 2026 is dated 10 April 2026 and states it is "in force immediately", but no commencement clause specific to §§62 and 94 was found. **Whether the rewritten s.88 reaches WPPF payouts made between 1 July 2025 and 10 April 2026 is unconfirmed.**

**F5. The Extension power conflict.** PwC states the **NBR's** power to extend the return filing date by one month is repealed. But **s.2(80A)(e)**, inserted by Act 89 of 2026, gives the **Commissioner of Taxes** power to extend an individual's specified date by up to **90 days** on prior written application. **Whether the Finance Act 2026 removed s.2(80A)(e) is unconfirmed.** These are different office-holders and different periods, so they may simply coexist.

**F6. Labour Rules 2015 citations are second-hand.** Rule 362(2)(a) (Form-80, 15 July) and Rule 362(2)(b) (Form-81, 15 February) come from a **third-party mirror** of the official English gazette text, not from bdlaws. **Factory licence renewal date, fire licence validity (1–3 years) and environmental clearance validity (1–2 years) are sourced only from commercial compliance-services websites** and are excluded from the calendar.

**F7. The old ITO 1984 Tk 50,000 WPPF exemption** (6th Sch Pt A para 21(d)) is cited from secondary sources only, for historical context. What is verified is the *absence* of any WPPF exemption paragraph in Sixth Schedule Part 1 of the Income Tax Act 2023.

**F8. No independent second reading of the Second Schedule** (provident, gratuity and superannuation funds) was obtained — a dedicated research stream on that area did not return, and the content rests on a single careful reading of NBR's authentic English text plus ACE Advisory.

**F9. KPMG's car perquisite table contains a typo** — overlapping "1500 cc to 2500 cc" and "2000 cc to 2500 cc" bands. Three other sources (bdlaws amending text, PwC WWTS, ACE) agree the second band is **1,500–2,000 cc**, which is what this document uses.

**F10. PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries is out of date on the mandatory provident fund trigger** — it states "at least three-fourths of the total number of workers" and omits the ≥100 permanent-worker threshold. The primary Labour Act text as amended in 2026 says **two-thirds** and **≥100 permanent workers**.

**G1. Given the 1 July 2026 commencement and the previous turnover-tax convention, the calendar assumes **Q1 = July–September, due 15 October**. **Confirm against an NBR General Order before diarising.**

**G2. The 31 December "audited financial statements to the VAT authority" date** derives from KPMG's "within 6 months after the end of each financial year" and assumes a 30 June financial year end. The underlying VAT-law provision was not identified.

**G3. Treat that source's other figures with caution; it was used here only for the two salary-statement rows, both flagged.

## H. Bookkeeping, accounting and audit — resolved conflicts and remaining gaps

### H(i) Errors resolved — but the wrong version is widely published

**H1. ITA 2023 s.73 audit thresholds — PwC is wrong by a factor of ten.** PwC Bangladesh's *Finance Act 2026: Key Amendments* prints "turnover exceeding **BDT 10m** or capital exceeding **BDT 5m**". The enacted Bangla text of **Finance Act 2026 s.59** says **১০ কোটি (Tk 10 crore = BDT 100,000,000)** and **৫ কোটি (Tk 5 crore = BDT 50,000,000)**. Verified twice — in the Finance Act 2026 itself and as substituted into ITA 2023 on bdlaws — and independently consistent with KPMG and Tuhin & Partners. **Use 10 crore / 5 crore.**

**H2. FRC "public interest entity" thresholds — the Tk 5 crore / 3 crore / 1 crore figures are OBSOLETE.** They come from FRC Notification 179/FRC/FRM/Prokgapon/2020-01 (11 March 2020, gazetted 29 June 2020) and are still quoted by law-firm blogs and 2020 press. **Superseded by S.R.O. 34/35/36-Ain/2023 (gazetted 16 February 2023): Tk 50 crore revenue / Tk 30 crore assets / Tk 10 crore liabilities excluding equity**, plus **50 persons** minimum manpower (Regulations gazetted 23 October 2023). FRC's own November 2025 IFRS Foundation presentation confirms the 2023 figures. Using the obsolete set would wrongly classify small companies as PIEs.

**H3. RJSC company late fee — "Tk 2 per day, maximum Tk 1,000" is OBSOLETE.** Replaced by **S.R.O. 101-Ain/2019 (23 April 2019)** with **Tk 500 per year or part (≤3 years) / Tk 700 per year (>3 years)**, unchanged by the 2023 amendment. The obsolete figure survives only in RJSC's stale English FAQ, which also shows pre-2016 charge-registration fees. **Do not cite that FAQ for any fee.**

**H4. Form XII deadline — 14 days, not 15.** RJSC's Bangla page says 15; **s.115(2) says 14** and RJSC's English FAQ agrees.

**H5. "Form 118" does not exist** in RJSC's catalogue — likely a confusion with Form 117 or an import from Indian practice.

**H6. BSEC auditor rotation is NOT in the Corporate Governance Code 2018.** It is in **BSEC Notification No. BSEC/CMRRCD/2006-158/208/Admin/81 of 20 June 2018 (gazetted 8 August 2018), conditions 2(2) and 2(3)**. Much commentary misattributes it.

**H7. FRC Notification 146/FRC/SS/Prokgapon/2020/71 (21 Dec 2020) is about the auditor's SIGNATURE format, not the DVC.** It does not mention DVS or DVC. Do not cite it as a DVC instrument.

**H8. Companies Act s.190(1) — the Bangla and English texts diverge.** The **Bangla enacted text** contains a proviso requiring a **private company to file the balance sheet and the profit and loss account SEPARATELY**; the 1995 authentic English translation (S.R.O. 177-Law, 1 October 1995) **omits it**. The Bangla is authoritative, and RJSC practice corroborates it. That English text also carries obvious typographical errors and predates every amendment — including ss.185(2A)/(2B), 190(1A) and 212(5) inserted by the FRA 2015, and the whole of Part X-A (One Person Company). **Use the Bangla text for the current law.**

**H9. Companies Act s.181(7)(a)** reads oddly in Bangla (literally "the manager and the manager's banker, auditor and legal advisers are excluded"). The authentic English resolves it: *"…and all officers **but excluding the bankers, auditors and legal advisers**"*. Use the English reading.

### H(ii) Could not verify

**H10. ⭐ The Bangla-or-English requirement for books of account does NOT appear to exist.** No express language requirement was found in the Companies Act 1994, the Income Tax Act 2023 or its rules, or the VAT & SD Act 2012 or Rules 2016. The "Bangla or English" formulation appears to originate in the **repealed VAT Rules 1991**, whose text could not be retrieved to confirm the rule number or wording. **If a Bangla-or-English rule is to be asserted for VAT records, it needs a citation that could not be supplied here.**

**H11. No express currency requirement** that accounts be maintained in Taka was found in any of the three statutes. Only indirect evidence exists (Schedules X and XI denominated in Taka; the ITA computes tax to the nearest Taka; IAS 21 applies as an adopted standard).

**H12. No standing FRC deadline for PIEs to file audited financial statements with the Council.** FRA s.45(3) says "in the manner prescribed by regulations" and s.45(4) allows case-by-case orders, but **no regulation prescribing a routine deadline was located**, and FRC's own November 2025 presentation says the PIE report submission portal is **still being developed**. **Do not state a 90- or 120-day FRC deadline.**

**H13. Whether RJSC requires a DVC on s.190 filings — UNPROVEN.** No RJSC notice, circular, reference number or commencement date was found, and no RJSC page fetched mentions DVC or DVS. The only verified RJSC link is the **22 December 2024** integration with the Name Clearance Portal, which ICAB describes as company data flowing **from RJSC into DVS** — a data lookup, not a filing gate. Widely asserted on advisory blogs.

**H14. Which SMEs / non-PIEs must apply IFRS for SMEs rather than full IFRS.** FRC adopted IFRS for SMEs in November 2020 for PIEs, and FRA ss.40(4) and 41 empower a separate simplified framework with small-entity exemptions — but **no FRC instrument setting quantified applicability criteria was found**. The IFRS Foundation's Bangladesh profile (September 2020) records it as "under review, not yet finalised"; nothing later was located.

**H15. Bangladesh Bank's reported July 2021 DVS circular** — the Financial Express (7 July 2021) reports it was issued on 6 July 2021, but a systematic search of bb.org.bd circulars across 4–7 July 2021 and the Wayback index produced **no hit**. Number, department and wording unconfirmed. (By contrast **BRPD Circular Letter No. 04 of 4 January 2021** *is* verified — but it does **not** mention DVS or DVC.)

**H16. BSEC has no located formal DVC directive** — only a Letter of Intent with ICAB reported for 10 March 2021. **BIDA's and MRA's DVS requirements** appear only in ICAB's own list of users; no instrument was found for either.

**H17. The FRC "DVC within 3 days of signing, 15 days exceptional" guideline** is sourced only to The Business Standard (25 March 2026); the guideline document itself was not found on frc.gov.bd.

**H18. The two-letter component of the DVC** (`AS` in the specimen) is undocumented; ICAB publishes no format specification, and the decode rests on **one observed specimen**. Similarly, every source says **five** key financial data are entered into DVS but only four are ever named (profit, turnover, total assets, liabilities).

**H19. No published ICAB fee for generating a DVC was found** anywhere. Commentary about "rising audit fees after DVS" refers to firms' fees to clients — do not conflate.

**H20. Foreign companies — there is NO annual-accounts deadline in the Act.** Companies Act s.380(1) says only "in every English calendar year"; neither the Act nor any RJSC page states a number of days. Applying "within 30 days of the AGM" to a foreign company would be an unsupported extrapolation from s.190. Likewise s.379(3) requires change returns "within the prescribed time" — **the prescribing rule was not located**.

**H21. Trade Organisation Rules 2025 content** could not be extracted (font-subset issue in the PDF). **Any periodic return required under the Trade Organisation Act 2022 / Rules 2025 is unresearched.**

**H22. Whether RJSC's per-year late fee is charged IN ADDITION TO or INSTEAD OF the Tk 200 base filing fee** is not stated expressly by either RJSC's page or the gazette; the page's layout implies additive.

**H23. Whether RJSC online filing is mandatory**, and whether a client-side digital signature certificate is required for e-filing, could not be established from a primary RJSC rule.

**H24. Current state of the IFRS roadmap for banks and NBFIs.** FRC's notification of 16 March 2020 mandated IFRS compliance for banks and financial institutions, and ICAB/FRC were working with Bangladesh Bank on a roadmap as at 2020. **Whether Bangladesh Bank has since withdrawn its IFRS departures is unverified**; FRC's November 2025 presentation is silent.

**H25. The NBR Special Order cites "section 184A" of the ITO 1984** as the authority for Income Tax Paripatra-1, 2018-19. But s.184A ITO 1984 is *"Requirement of twelve-digit Taxpayer's Identification Number in certain cases"*, which has no evident connection to issuing a paripatra. Verified twice against the scan; could not be reconciled, and Paripatra-1 2018-19 could not be located. **Reproduce as printed, but treat it as a probable error in the original order.**

**H26. ICAB's DVC volume figures do not reconcile.** The same Daily Star article (20 March 2024) reports **38,316** DVCs "in 2021-22" and, in the next sentence, **29,000** "in the last financial year" — implying a ~24% fall, contradicting the article's own statement that 38,316 was "nearly 2 percent" up year on year. **H27. When FRC came on board with DVS** — press describes FRC arrangements from 2021, but **FRC's own website dates the DVS MoU to 14 September 2025**. Probably an informal arrangement followed by a formal MoU, but no FRC document evidencing a 2021 arrangement was found.

**H28. ICAB's own metadata contains errors** — its website records the NBR Special Order news item with a publish date of 26 October 2020 although the order is dated 26 November 2020, and its transcription mis-renders the MoU date as "১২ নভেম্বর ২০১০" where the scan clearly reads ২০২০. Trust the scanned original.

**H29. Several RJSC forms carry internal citation errors** — Form XII's printed title omits "CHANGE"; Form XVIII's header cites ss.159 and 391 while its body says "Sections 169 & 391"; RJSC's pages cite "Section 12 & 391" for Form XXVIII where the form says ss.172 and 391; and RJSC's *society* name-change and address-change forms wrongly recite "section 60 of the partnership Act, IX of 1932".
